# ISS LLC + TCAF — Canonical Enterprise Campaign and Orchestration Plan

**Owner:** Dr. Terry D. Flood

**Version:** 1.0 — October 21, 2026  
**Status:** Controlling enterprise campaign plan. Earlier ORCHESTRATION reports supply evidence and subordinate detail; they do not override this plan.

## 1. Commander's intent

Build one evidence-controlled enterprise in which every owned platform can stand alone, earn revenue or funding, and deliver a measurable outcome, while also contributing to a larger system that turns community, government, health, education, resilience, and defense signals into verified decisions, funded execution, receipts, outcomes, and learning.

The 18-month minimum capital objective is **$5 million for ISS LLC and $5 million for TCAF**. This is a planning objective, not a forecast or guarantee. ISS and TCAF remain legally, financially, operationally, and evidentially separate. Client grant amounts processed through GrantPathPro are not ISS revenue; only contracted fees, licenses, services, subscriptions, or other consideration earned by ISS count toward the ISS target.

## 3. Enterprise end state at 18 months

By April 2028:

1. GitHub is the single source of truth for every owned codebase; no unpublished Replit-only product change exists.
2. Every marketed platform has one canonical repository, one owner entity, one authoritative production identity, one claim ledger, one sustainment owner, and a verified release path.
3. GrantPathPro is the evidence-qualified pursuit and proposal operating system for ISS, TCAF, and authorized clients.
4. ISS has at least $5 million in booked revenue, awarded non-dilutive funding, or executed contract value attributable to ISS; the categories are reported separately.
5. TCAF has at least $5 million in awarded grants, charitable commitments, program contracts, or executed subawards attributable to TCAF; the categories are reported separately.
6. The broader client portfolio may submit or manage substantially more than $10 million through GrantPathPro, but client request volume is not counted as enterprise revenue or awards.
7. Three repeatable market motions have referenceable evidence: defense/emergency partner prototypes, public/community implementation programs, and evidence-qualified proposal operations.
8. Every external action follows signal → verification → qualification → preparation → approval → execution → receipt → outcome → learning.

## 6. Lines of operation

These are sequential campaign operations; several lines of effort run through every operation.

1. **Reconcile and contain (now–December 2026):** close branch drift, secrets, privacy/security defects, deployment identity gaps, and stale pursuits; complete registration preflights.
2. **Package and position (November 2026–January 2027):** turn verified capabilities into three offers per priority platform—pilot, implementation, and enterprise/agency—each with scope, price logic, evidence, limits, and acceptance criteria.
3. **Validate beachheads (January–April 2027):** run bounded pilots in defense/emergency management, Texas community implementation, and GrantPathPro-assisted pursuit operations.
4. **Capture and compete (February–September 2027):** pursue verified grants, contracts, OTAs, SBIR/STTR, subcontracts, foundations, and program-service agreements using capture plans prepared 90–180 days before deadlines.
5. **Deliver and prove (April–December 2027):** implement awards/pilots, capture delivery/receipt/outcome evidence, publish only approved case evidence, and convert successful delivery into renewals and references.
6. **Scale and sustain (October 2027–April 2028):** expand repeatable offers, renew contracts, add qualified geographies and partners, retire weak products or campaigns, and allocate resources by measured return.

## 8. Capital architecture

The minimum **ISS $5M** planning mix is: approximately $2.0M government contracts/OTAs/subcontracts; $1.25M product pilots, enterprise licenses, and implementation services; $1.0M SBIR/STTR or other non-dilutive R&D; and $0.75M strategic/private capital or partner-funded development. The minimum **TCAF $5M** planning mix is: approximately $2.0M government grants/program contracts; $1.5M foundation/corporate philanthropy; $1.0M coalition/subaward/fiscal-sponsor work; and $0.5M program service or mission-aligned earned revenue. These are portfolio construction targets, not expected awards.

Maintain at least **4× qualified pipeline coverage**: $20M of verified executable ISS opportunities for a $5M floor, and $20M of verified executable TCAF opportunities for a $5M floor. A source signal, forecast, or attractive listing does not count. Pipeline value is weighted only after authoritative status, eligibility, entity fit, capability evidence, capacity, submission route, and decision ownership are verified.

## 9. Winning-proposal system

Winning begins before writing. For each target, GrantPathPro builds a capture record containing the buyer/funder mission, problem, acquisition/funding mechanism, incumbent/current solution, decision calendar, stakeholders, likely competitors, teaming need, evaluation criteria, compliance burden, evidence gaps, and win themes. The proposal is produced only after a bid/no-bid decision.

Every package contains: authoritative solicitation and amendments; compliance matrix; entity and registration proof; requirements-to-capability crosswalk; named gaps and mitigation; technical/program design; staffing and partner workshare; schedule; budget/pricing; risk and sustainment; past performance; measurable outcomes; required forms; red-team findings; exact-text approval; submission receipt. Boilerplate is reusable only when its source, owner, date, and claim status remain current.

The proposal cadence is **180/120/90/60/30/14/7/2-day planning**. Relationship and teaming work begins 90–180 days out; solution and evidence architecture by 60–90 days; first compliant draft by 30 days; red team by 14; forms/portal preflight by 7; final authority and upload rehearsal by 2. Late opportunities are accepted only when a written exception shows that quality and compliance remain achievable.

## 10. Marketing strategy

The portfolio is not marketed as one generic AI suite. Use one enterprise narrative—**evidence-controlled implementation from signal to outcome**—with distinct buyer motions:

- SHIELD | ATLAS: direct account capture, primes/integrators, consortiums, capability demonstrations, and mission-specific technical briefs.
- GrantPathPro: founder-led demonstrations, multi-client pilots, proposal-office partnerships, municipal/HBCU/nonprofit cohorts, and evidence-qualified case studies.
- ThriveUp: coalition development, community briefings, government/foundation program design, implementation partnerships, and place-based outcome reports.
- FinLitSpark: Texas school, workforce, employer, banking, and community cohorts with adult/child boundaries and measured learning/adoption.
- Health, veteran, child, violence, hazard, and civic products: partnership-first campaigns until privacy, deployment, claims, and delivery receipts support broader promotion.

Channels have different jobs: direct outreach builds relationships; LinkedIn establishes professional proof; webinars and demonstrations reduce buyer uncertainty; partner events create access; email nurtures known stakeholders; search/content captures active demand; paid media is a controlled experiment only after the offer, audience, deployment, attribution, budget owner, and stop rule are ready. No campaign uses an unsupported capability or treats impressions as outcomes.

## 13. Software occupation and release rules

1. Codex is the only routine code writer across GitHub and opens one focused branch per purpose from current canonical `main`.
2. Replit is read/publish/runtime only. No independent coding or automatic unpublished product commits.
3. Vercel is a build/hosting provider where applicable, not source authority.
4. Every PR must identify purpose, owner, inputs, outputs, failure state, tests, expected state delta, rollback, and limits.
5. Required gates are exact-head typecheck, targeted tests, full relevant suite, security/privacy checks, product-specific gates, and an exact-head review.
6. Merge does not mean deployed. Deployment needs environment, deployment ID, status, timestamp, exact revision/build manifest, smoke test, and rollback path.
7. Merged and superseded branches are deleted only after their unique commits and required evidence are reconciled.
8. Stale branches are compared by patch and intent; do not rebase them blindly. Preserve unique value in a new current-main branch or close them with a reason.
9. Secrets never enter Git history. A matched value must be rotated when exposure cannot be disproved.
10. Every release reports **Before / Changed / Why / Proof / Limits**.

## 16. Measures and decision rules

**Measures of performance:** source/deployment agreement; exact-head gate pass rate; lead-to-qualified-opportunity time; duplicate/stale rejection; registration closure; proposal on-time/compliance rate; relationship follow-through; campaign conversion; delivery fidelity; cost and burden.

**Measures of effectiveness:** awarded/executed value by entity; renewal and conversion; time or cost saved for clients; service access or participant outcome where ethically measured; agency/partner action after delivery; sustained use; unintended harm; distribution/equity effects. Activity, submissions, meetings, builds, and impressions never substitute for these outcomes.

Stop or adapt when: source/deployment identity is unresolved; claim exceeds evidence; eligibility or submission route is unknown; unit economics fail the preset threshold; response quality is low after the defined sample; a partner cannot supply essential workshare; privacy/security controls fail; or implementation evidence shows the offer is not producing the intended outcome.
