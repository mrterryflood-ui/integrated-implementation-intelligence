# DIS–RPLICE ChainWeb | Comparative Implementation-Science Methods Validation

**Research status:** evidence preflight complete; matched comparative exercise prepared, NOT conducted; no independent rater outcomes, clinical impact, or method-superiority finding.  
**Date:** 2026-11-06. **Citations:** AMA style. **Human review before any external communication.**

## Primary question and explicit separation

Does supplementing **competent** current implementation-science practice with DIS ChainWeb improve (a) correct identification of material, evidence-supported failure mechanisms, (b) selection of context-fit corrective actions with core fidelity preserved, and (c) sustainment decisions—without added unsupported claims, time, cost, or inequity?

**Research intervention:** ChainWeb-supported implementation decision-making, **not** colonoscopy, navigation or cancer therapy. Evidence-based interventions such as mailed FIT and navigation provide source-based challenge cases. The user’s clinical-health research and live healthcare platforms are separate workstreams.

## Comparator is not a straw man

**Control:** skilled analysts using the updated CFIR (2022), iterative PRISM–RE-AIM, FRAME-IS, the 2026 learning-health-system improvement pillars and steps, eligible epidemiological methods and actual stakeholder consideration. The control has the same case facts, reference access, time window, staffing, and usable AI privileges as the ChainWeb arm.

**Experiment:** all control capabilities PLUS ChainWeb’s versioned evidence-bound node/edge map, quantified missingness, independent rival explanations, root-cause discrimination tests, cross-system effects, explicit owner/action/outcome chain, and day-after-success and long-range learning triggers. RPLICE specialist methods are opt-in via a real human’s selection, valid provider credentials and specific authorized inputs. Selection alone is not execution.

The question is **incremental value over the best existing approach**. An implementation plan longer than the comparator is not by itself a better one.

## Source-grounded challenge cases

The local evidence casebook contains eight published studies/analyses with nine separately defined numerical comparisons:

| Case | Design and authentic outcome | Evidence boundary |
|---|---|---|
| NC centralized FIT + navigation (Reuland et al 2024) | RCT; 6-month CRC screening 30.0% vs 9.7%, adjusted difference 20.29 pp (95% CI 17.85–22.73) | Positive-FIT colonoscopy secondary contrast includes zero; no demonstrated mortality reduction |
| US multi-cancer overdue follow-up (Atlas et al 2023) | Cluster RCT; reminders/outreach/navigation vs usual care: 31.4% vs 22.9% at 120 d, adjusted +8.5 pp (95% CI 4.8–12.0) | Extra navigation vs active outreach +0.4 pp (95% CI −3.6 to 4.2); not proof of extra benefit or zero effect |
| WA post-abnormal FIT navigation (Coronado et al 2025) | RCT; 1-y follow-up colonoscopy 55.1% vs 42.1%, difference +13.0 pp (95% CI 6.5–19.4) | Diagnostic closure, not new cancer prevention |
| OR rural Medicaid mailed FIT (Coronado et al 2025) | Cluster RCT; 6-month screening 11.8% vs 4.5%, adjusted +7.3 pp (95% CI 5.3–9.2) | FIT mail coverage was 1489/1489 eligible after screening; navigation 11/19 positive-FIT cases—different denominators |
| CA three-year screening (Martinez et al 2026) | Cluster RCT; year 2 difference +10.8 pp (95% CI 4.9–16.7), year 3 +2.9 pp (95% CI −3.2–9.0) | Intervention rate rose 37.8%→39.4%; usual care rose 27.0%→36.5%; changing cohorts and earlier eligibility query prohibit attribution to fidelity alone |
| CA AltaMed navigation (Tangka et al 2025) | Observational; FIT return 36.6%→51.0% between distinct annual cohorts | No randomized counterfactual; costs, workflow and follow-up remain important |
| Boston PCOS diagnosis (Silva et al 2024/25) | Observational missed diagnosis association | No diagnostic-intervention result or state prevalence inference |
| FRAME-IS multilevel case (Garza et al 2026) | Implementation-methods application in 32 clinics | Evidence established approaches already capture adaptation; demonstrates challenges with cascading/nested modification |

No effect-size pooling is permitted across unlike outcomes/periods. None proves ChainWeb works.

## Reproducible preflight performed

- Public casebook: **8 cases / 9 published contrasts**, from original cited publications; selected sample, **not** systematic review or independent row-level meta-analysis.
- Evidence-validator checks: case uniqueness; named intervention and comparator; source and time; RCT/observational distinction; confidence intervals; conditional fidelity denominators; no overclaim about null, causality, transport, clinical deployment or ChainWeb superiority.
- Local tests: **16 checks passed** across case integrity and matched packet creation.
- Matched analyst assignments: **16 packets**, two methods per case; identical case data and common question for each pair. Fixed seed **20261106**. The condition mapping is kept separate from the reviewer templates.
- **No actual analysts or independent blinded reviewers** have submitted outputs. No validated gold failure-mechanism labels are available, and the comparison is **NOT EXECUTED**. Packet preflight is not a clinical or scientific victory.

## Independent validation design, prespecified for review

1. Freeze original evidence and expert-assessed hidden holdout cases with separate source/version hashes.
2. Obtain institutional determination about human-subjects status, data access, analyst recruitment and ethics as appropriate.
3. Assemble qualified independent analysts; use randomly allocated case/order and balanced methods training. Preserve equal evidence and effort budgets.
4. Generate both arms’ decision briefs from the same cases, masking condition labels before blinded external rating. Record whether reviewers correctly guessed the arm.
5. Independent experts adjudicate reference gaps and clinically unsafe claims. Primary endpoints: **material failure-mechanism recall** and **unsupported material-claim rate**. Secondary: intervention-fit accuracy, adaptation/fidelity, decision clarity, equity, burden and time/cost. Compare at the case level with rater agreement, sensitivity to site/condition, and multiplicity controls.
6. Register primary tests and meaningful effect thresholds BEFORE evaluative outcomes are opened; include prospective unseen cases and negative controls. A favorable result from 8 known cases alone does not support clinical transfer.
7. Advance to a real, authorized field evaluation only if scientific, legal, operational and resource gates are independently met. Monitor adoption, delivery quality, patient/worker burden, fidelity, adaptation and long-term maintenance.
