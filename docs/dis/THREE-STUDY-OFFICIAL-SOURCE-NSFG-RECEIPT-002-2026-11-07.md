# Original-source audit receipt 002 — NSFG women's health
2026-11-07. Public official-source review, **not** raw microdata replication. Source: Nugent CN, Lochner KA. NCHS Health E-Stat 121 (July 2026), https://www.cdc.gov/nchs/data/hestat/hestat121.htm DOI: 10.15620/cdc/252459.
## Directly verified against official publication
2022–2023 National Survey of Family Growth, 4,856 female respondents ages 20–49. Estimates represent U.S. household population of women ages 20–49; survey weights and complex sample design applied using SUDAAN 11.0.3. Ever-diagnosed endometriosis 6.2% (95% CI 5.2–7.2), PCOS 8.7% (7.7–9.8), fibroids 7.8% (6.8–8.9). These are not independently verified biological prevalence or state-specific estimates.
### Recognition and selection signals
- Endometriosis ever-diagnosed: non-Hispanic White 8.1%, non-Hispanic Black 3.7%; higher family income >=300% FPL 7.1% vs <150% FPL 4.4%. Source flags specific statistical comparisons; no adjusted cause is established.
- PCOS ever-diagnosed: non-Hispanic Black 4.4% versus non-Hispanic White 9.9%. Source notes Black group is significantly different from other groups shown, but does not establish whether this is biological prevalence, diagnostic access or reporting.
- Fibroids ever-diagnosed: non-Hispanic Black 12.5%, non-Hispanic White 7.1%; private insurance 8.8% versus uninsured 5.2%. Source flags the insurance comparison as significant; access mechanism is a hypothesis, not demonstrated.
- Fertility problems: endometriosis diagnosed 33.0% vs not diagnosed 18.6%; PCOS 45.2% vs 17.1%; fibroids 25.4% vs 19.0%. These are bivariate cross-sectional associations and may be confounded or reflect selection and reporting.
- Ever fertility-service use: endometriosis 30.7% vs 12.7%; PCOS 34.5% vs 11.7%; fibroids 31.4% vs 12.3%. Service use does not measure need fulfillment, diagnostic quality or fertility benefit.
## What is supported
The official report demonstrates statistically identified differences in *reported diagnosis* across demographic/insurance groups and differences in fertility problems and service utilization associated with diagnosis. It cannot determine which mechanisms explain those differences. Lower ever-diagnosed percentage is not proof of lower underlying occurrence, and higher fertility-service use is not proof of better outcomes.
## Next discriminating studies
1. Reproduce NSFG estimates using public-use microdata, complex design weights and published codebook; report design-correct uncertainty, suppressed cells and subgroup sample sizes.
2. Test age and insurance confounding, diagnostic opportunity, symptoms, health-care utilization and self-report measurement limitations. Stratified and adjusted analyses require appropriately defined estimands and care about selection bias.
3. Independently identify clinically ascertained condition-specific population studies; evaluate endometriosis confirmation and verification bias, PCOS diagnostic definition variation, and fibroid imaging/symptom ascertainment.
4. Interview or otherwise collect ethically authorized lived-experience evidence on recognition, diagnostic delay, referral, costs and harms. Do not invent stakeholder testimony.
5. Identify interventions only after mechanism discrimination. Candidate service-process measures: time from first symptom report to appropriate evaluation, unresolved referrals, diagnostic uncertainty and treatment burden; clinical partners must define appropriateness.
