# DIS whole-system research-to-action exercise: Jackson → Houston
**Version:** 2026-11-06 · **Claim level:** documented analytic exercise, **not** implemented field pilot, verified stakeholder interviews or demonstrated clinical effect.

## 1. Mission, end state and competing definitions of success
**Question:** What would prevent avoidable late-stage colorectal cancer diagnoses among eligible populations in and around Jackson, Mississippi, and which mechanisms might transfer to Houston, Texas without assuming the same intervention will work the same way?

**Intended end state:** equitable, sustained access to appropriate screening and clinically indicated diagnostic evaluation, with fewer avoidable lapses, fewer untreated abnormal results, reliable handoffs, better experience and verifiable improvements in relevant health outcomes. Keep screening uptake, diagnostic timeliness and late-stage incidence as *different* measurements, not interchangeable proof.

**Observed starting facts (NCI State Cancer Profiles, 2018–2022):**
| Geography | Age-adjusted late-stage colorectal incidence per 100,000 (95% CI) | Percentage of diagnosed colorectal cancers classified late-stage | Average annual late-stage count |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Hinds County, Mississippi | **30.4** (27.3–33.8) | **63.3%** | 76 |
| Harris County, Texas | **23.3** (22.7–24.0) | **63.4%** | 1,044 |

Hinds County **is not Jackson city**; Harris County **is not Houston city**. Different denominator definitions and geographic composition matter. Higher Hinds late-stage incidence with similar late-stage proportions raises competing explanations—including underlying incidence, demographic composition, screening, diagnostic timing and measurement—not a conclusion that a particular institution failed. Annual counts should not be compared as if populations were equally sized.

**Evidence inference:** multicomponent screening interventions and patient navigation have evidence for improving screening uptake. That evidence does **not** establish reduced late-stage incidence from a particular local configuration. Clinical practice, referral, and local effectiveness must be independently evaluated.

## 2. Designed, operational and experienced realities
| Lens | What should be investigated | Status |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Designed | Screening eligibility, reminders, navigation pathways, abnormal-result follow-up, diagnostic capacity, privacy and policy intended to ensure access | Existing clinical standards and EBIs can inform a planned map; local protocols not verified |
| Operational | Actual appointment slots, Medicaid/uninsured handling, closed-loop referrals, path turnaround, staff capacity, transport links, nonresponse rates, missed handoffs | **Unknown pending authorized facility/workflow data** |
| Experienced | How patients, family caregivers, CHWs, primary care teams and diagnostic staff encounter delays, costs, trust, language, accessibility and consent | **Not interviewed or independently confirmed** |

**System question:** Is the mechanism of concern screening access, diagnostic closure, symptom evaluation, underlying disease risk, or more than one? Different mechanisms require different interventions and different causal claims.

## 3. Competing mechanism hypotheses and falsification
1. **Screening access:** eligible people are less likely to be offered or complete screening. Disconfirm using guideline-specific, age-/risk-adjusted eligible denominators; audit outreach, uptake, opt-out and case-mix.
2. **Abnormal-result closure:** positive tests or concerning symptoms do not receive clinically appropriate diagnostic evaluation quickly. Disconfirm using deidentified, lawful, result-to-resolution events and cross-facility closure records.
3. **Structural burden:** transport, work hours, affordability, disability access, language or childcare prevent follow-through. Test through consented patient interviews, observed appointment access and structured barrier data; avoid assuming any group's experience.
4. **Capacity/quality:** colonoscopy, specialist, pathology or primary care capacity changes the interval to diagnosis. Test appointment queues, true available capacity, timely completed encounters and acuity.
5. **Biology / composition / observation:** changes reflect disease biology, stage coding, registry completeness, total incidence, age structure, geography or small-cell instability. Test surveillance metadata and competing case-mix models.
6. **Program interference:** more screening could initially identify more disease, including late stage. Do not treat a transient increase in detected cases as evidence the intervention failed.

Each hypothesis requires a qualified owner, original records, a discriminating measure and a red-team alternative explanation.

## 4. Stakeholder and authority map (roles proposed, nobody falsely enrolled)
- **People eligible for screening or diagnostic follow-up:** lived experience, affordability, preferences, access burden; genuine participation only after consent.
- **Community health workers and navigators:** referral and social-support transitions; may perceive barriers different from administrative reports.
- **Primary care, gastroenterology, pathology and oncology staff:** clinical quality, capacity, accurate eligibility and appropriate triage; clinical decisions remain under licensed authority.
- **Community organizations and trusted messengers:** legitimacy, translation, accessibility and sustained engagement; no partner is represented as committed.
- **Health systems, payers, Medicaid and public-health offices:** incentives, coverage, care coordination, reporting, confidentiality and accountable resources.
- **Methodologist, registry scientist, evaluator and patient representative:** denominator/DDD validity, adverse effects, equity and independent challenge.
- **Successor program manager / funder:** day-after-success service continuity, maintenance, recurrent cost and long-term exit criteria.

RPLICE Parallax may compare **operator-declared** viewpoints after HIL selection and consent. It does not impersonate or replace actual testimony. Stakeholder power, conflict, burden and missing voices belong in the consultation register.

## 5. Candidate EBI, adaptations and SALP (method is HIL-selected)
**Candidate core function to test:** enable timely, guideline-concordant screening and clinically appropriate resolution of abnormal findings with clear ownership and patient-centered navigation.

**Possible delivery forms:** mailed stool-test offers, phone/text reminders where consented, community navigator contact, clinic scheduling support, extended hours, language services, ride coordination. These forms are hypotheses requiring local workflow fit and resource validation. Neither Jackson nor Houston delivery method is preset.

**Human-selected RPLICE analytical methods:** SALP for structural anchors and leverage; CFIR for determinants; RE-AIM for reach/adoption/implementation/maintenance; Parallax for declared perspective differences. Record chosen method, owner, purpose, data-sharing consent, tool version, date, source and provisional output. **Never run all methodologies automatically.**

**Proposed SALP anchors (unweighted until a human chooses):** reliable diagnostic referral capacity; closed-loop follow-up; trust and accessible contact; coverage/affordability; transport and scheduling; operational ownership; data quality and interoperable handoffs. Candidate leverage: a reliable named owner and cross-setting feedback loop for abnormal-result resolution. This is a *working hypothesis*, not a verified causal center of gravity.

## 6. Implementation logic and evidence-dependent SMART pilot
**Inputs:** site and community partners with real agreements; funding and personnel, licensed clinical capacity, privacy/data steward, clinical and navigational training, secure measures, referral resources, implementation evaluator.

**Activities:** co-design eligibility and referral pathways; rehearse consent and data flow; train staff; map handoffs; test navigator/clinic scheduling and positive-test closure; review exceptions weekly.

**Outputs:** verified eligible-denominator registry; initiated contacts, appropriate screening offers, completed screening and medically indicated resolution records; consented journey feedback; staffing utilization and cost log.

**Outcomes:** proximal access and follow-up gains, equitable reach, acceptable staff burden and safe time-to-diagnostic closure. Sustained changes in cancer stage at diagnosis require longer observation and suitable data; do not use short-term screening uptake as proof of reduced late-stage disease.

**Example SMART development (proposed targets, not approved baselines):**
| Measure | Numerator / denominator | Proposed temporal decision | Owner | Balancing metric |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Appropriate screening completion | Completed screening among persons verified eligible within agreed window / all eligible persons in that window | Compare a verified 90-day prepilot baseline with pilot period at 30/60/90 days | Clinical quality lead | False eligibility and patient burden |
| Positive-result follow-up | Positive tests receiving clinically appropriate documented resolution in site-defined timeframe / all actionable positives | Measure weekly and monthly; target set **after** baseline and clinician review | Diagnostic referral lead | Unnecessary testing, delays, safety |
| Equitable program reach | Engaged eligible persons by appropriately collected strata / eligible persons in each stratum | Monitor monthly, stratified only where permitted and meaningful | Community co-lead | Exclusion, consent and missingness |
| Navigator feasibility / sustainment | Completed useful navigation episodes, FTE time and cost / appropriate referrals | Review at 30/60/90 days; establish cost ceiling in approved budget | Program sponsor | Workforce burnout, sustainability |

No arbitrary improvement percentages are accepted as achieved goals; local sponsors must set realistic targets after baseline/resource assessments.

## 7. Pilot design and execution sequence
**Planning horizon — proposed, not launched:** days 0–30: source verification, local protocol/stakeholder consultation, IRB/QI determination, privacy/DUA, capacity map and baseline definition. Days 31–60: co-design, implementation planning, staffing/resource agreements, system rehearsal and incident escalation test. Days 61–150: bounded implementation and weekly monitoring if and only if authorized; collect delivery/fidelity receipts. Days 151–180: evaluative synthesis, independent review and human decisions on revise/stop/scale.

**Comparison selection:** a staggered rollout or appropriately matched comparison site may be feasible; selection depends on actual partner agreements and data. Prevent contamination, ethically unacceptable denial of standard care, compositional changes and time-varying confounding. No site is assigned treatment here.

**Risk/stop conditions:** clinical harm, privacy breach, invalid denominator, service capacity overflow, inequitable exclusion, recurring unclosed referrals, excessive workforce burden, partner withdrawal or unaffordable ongoing cost. The appropriate site authorities—not an AI output—own launch, pause and clinical decisions.

## 8. Difference-in-differences, DDD and negative evidence
The cited Hinds/Harris rates represent **one historical cross-sectional interval**. There are no verified pre/post clinic outcomes, no treatment exposure map, no valid comparative control, and no third dimension observed. **DDD estimate: not estimable.** Creating a numerical treatment effect from these county figures would be methodologically invalid.

A future observed panel must include:
- treated and comparison settings with justified comparability;
- pre- and post-intervention periods with documented treatment timing;
- focus and nonfocus subgroup definitions that remain stable;
- eight unique treatment × period × subgroup cells, consistent outcomes and valid denominators;
- sufficient pre-periods to assess trends, clustering, simultaneous policy changes, missing data, selection and spillovers;
- clinical/public-health ethics and precision/power planning.

The DIS demonstration tests the arithmetic only with **synthetic test fixtures**, visibly labeled synthetic. Synthetic coefficient ≠ local effect. A panel that merely supplies eight numbers is not automatically a causal design.

## 9. Jackson-to-Houston transportability test
**Retain:** the hypothesis about core functions (reliable timely clinical closure, appropriate screening and patient-centered navigation), and commitment to evaluate outcomes.

**Reinvestigate in Houston:** population/catchment and race/ethnicity strata without ecological inference; outpatient and diagnostic delivery capacity; payer/coverage context; health-system referral interoperability; geography/travel; community trust and language; workforce and cost; data access and surveillance definitions; social-service infrastructure. Harris County data are a **context signal**, not proof that the source-site pilot will transfer.

**Decision:** local evaluation and human selection of adaptation before implementation. It would be improper to declare Houston a replication merely because the same EBI name appears in the plan.

## 10. Whole-system 19-phase evidence trail
The existing DIS journey, reused without reimplementation, is:
1. STELLAR 1: fact-finding orientation; actual mission, context and counterfactual.
2. ORIENT: end state, risk and accountable mission owner.
3. EVIDENCE: source-by-claim critical appraisal and contrary research.
4. POPULATION: case definitions, geography, equity strata, denominators.
5. CAUSES: rival explanations and falsification instruments.
6. COMMUNITY: real participation and authority map, not invented partners.
7. SALP: HIL-selected anchors/leverage analysis via RPLICE.
8. EBI_FIT: core mechanism, intervention alternatives and local fit.
9. LOGIC: SMART model, meaningful measures, unintended effects.
10. GOVERNANCE: actual ethics, access, security and accountability.
11. PLAN: owners, resources, rehearsal and rollback.
12. PILOT: defensible baseline/comparator and feasibility.
13. IMPLEMENT: site-authenticated delivery, dates and fidelity receipts.
14. MONITOR: reach, burden, safety and quality by time and population.
15. EVALUATE: outcomes vs credible counterfactual, uncertainty and cost.
16. ADAPT: distinguish fidelity-preserving changes from drift.
17. COMMUNICATE: approved, comprehensible, accessible stakeholder feedback.
18. TRANSLATE: training, practice transfer, replication limits.
19. SCALE/SUSTAIN: locally validated transport, successor budget and re-map.

**Current code state:** all nineteen design stages can be traversed; none has a verified real-world pilot completion receipt. Each stage retains responsible roles and evidence requirements. No source citations can convert an unperformed field step into actual execution.

## 11. Rehearsal and longitudinal stewardship
**If early measures improve:** test whether eligibility denominator, coding, documentation and selection changed; independently validate outcomes and patient experience before celebrating a causal success. Review whether increased detection temporarily changes case rates.

**If partial success:** investigate whether reach improved while abnormal-result follow-up failed; ensure the intervention does not merely move the bottleneck downstream.

**If no change:** challenge instrument validity, implementation fidelity, patient preferences and comparison assumptions before discarding the mechanism.

**If harm or inequity emerges:** invoke accountable safety response and independent review; reverse or redesign the service, do not automatically scale a harmful process.

**Day after success:** identify who runs navigation tomorrow, pays recurring costs, owns unresolved referrals, maintains privacy and data systems, responds to complaints and trains successors.

**10 years later:** reassess screening guidance, geography, population, financing, clinical technologies, costs, access gaps, data definitions and institutional handoffs. Re-map the system when context changes, regardless of whether the first pilot met its targets.

## 12. Cross-domain transfer of reasoning
The portable element is **DIS's evidence-to-action decision architecture**, not a clinical EBI or a one-size intervention. In education, an outcome might be reading comprehension with a defined instrument; in workforce, verified placement and retention; in public safety, legitimate, measured service outcomes; in business, retained customer value and operating cost. Each domain substitutes appropriate sources, stakeholders, interventions, causal designs, rights and measures, while preserving MAP–GAP, HIL method selection, independent dissent, honest execution status and temporal stewardship.

**Final exercise verdict:** A defensible, reusable full-lifecycle analytical case exists. Causal effectiveness, signed partner participation, real pilot outcomes, authenticated live RPLICE method calls and production deployment are **not proven** by this document. Their proof must come from the relevant real-world and technical acceptance receipts.
