# DIS population-health investigation: prevention, counterfactuals and GIS
**Evidence snapshot:** November 6, 2026. **State:** source-informed analytic design and software prototype; not an implemented clinical or environmental intervention.

## What success means
The principal question is how to lower the occurrence of new invasive colorectal cancer. Survival, mortality, early detection and follow-up are indispensable complementary outcomes, but cannot replace observed incidence. Removing precancerous adenomas through guideline-concordant screening may prevent some new cancers; simply diagnosing existing invasive cancers earlier is not the same mechanism.

Define and measure at least four distinct constructs: (1) age-standardized incidence of all new invasive colorectal cancer in an eligible population, (2) new late-stage cancer incidence in population, (3) percentage of diagnosed cases classified regional/distant stage, and (4) mortality. Use each metric's own valid denominator, period, staging definition, uncertainty and population.

## The source observation and its limitations
NCI/CDC State Cancer Profiles 2018–2022 report Hinds County, Mississippi late-stage CRC incidence 30.4 per 100,000 annually (95% CI 27.3–33.8), annual count 76, and 63.3% diagnosed late; Harris County, Texas 23.3 (22.7–24.0), count 1,044, and 63.4% late. The percentages look virtually identical but the population-adjusted incidence rates differ approximately 30%. Two percentages do not constitute statistical correlation or prove equivalence. Hinds County is not Jackson city; Harris County is not Houston city.

Expanded 2018–2022 county comparison includes Dallas, Travis, Fort Bend, Cook, Will, Kane, Fulton, Gwinnett, DeKalb, Los Angeles, San Diego, Orange CA, and Mobile AL. Every rate has a source URL, 95% CI and average annual count in the typed population-health-atlas evidence register. Predeclare comparisons rather than choosing only striking similarities. The default comparator minimum is 100 late-stage cases annually during the five-year period; retain Hinds separately as the original sentinel, not a member of the large-county comparator set. This cutoff is a stability screen, not a sample-size or precision guarantee for subgroup analyses.

Sources: https://statecancerprofiles.cancer.gov/incidencerates/ and https://gis.cancer.gov/canceratlas/ . Official NCI Atlas all-stage incidence and subsite/time stratification have not yet been ingested into the new DIS local viewer; all-stage incidence cannot be inferred by dividing age-standardized late-stage incidence by a non-age-standardized late-stage share.

## Designed, operational and experienced realities
**Designed:** cancer prevention, risk reduction, guideline-concordant screening, appropriate polyp removal, timely clinically indicated diagnostic follow-up, coordinated referral and protected data use.
**Operational:** what actually occurs in nutrition and activity environments, tobacco/alcohol exposure, family-history risk assessment, coverage, patient outreach, primary care, endoscopy, pathology, navigation, clinical appointment capacity, surveillance data and payment incentives. These require official and partner-specific observation; no source here establishes these site operations.
**Experienced:** how affected adults, family caregivers, clinicians, community health workers and community organizations encounter trust, scheduling, travel, consent, language and cost; no interviews have been represented as completed.

## Predisposition, causation and environmental hypotheses
Established or strongly supported susceptibility factors include age, first-degree family history, hereditary Lynch syndrome and familial adenomatous polyposis, inflammatory bowel disease, and personal history of high-risk adenomas. Modifiable factors include smoking, heavy alcohol use, obesity, physical inactivity and aspects of diet; patterns and biological mechanisms differ by age/cohort and colon-versus-rectal subsite. Race and ethnicity are not sufficient biological causes, and a county percentage cannot identify who personally developed cancer or received an exposure. Younger birth cohorts and early-onset colorectal cancer require separate consideration.

The EPA ECHO map reports regulated facility locations and compliance context, not resident chemical doses, lifelong residential history, cancer clusters or attributable incidence. Environmental cause hypotheses must establish chemical/pathway plausibility, emissions and exposure windows, measured dose, geographic fit and latency, appropriate counterfactual and confounding control. Nearby facilities are leads for investigating exposure, not evidence of colorectal carcinogenic harm.

## Relevant local context without ecological inference
U.S. Census QuickFacts latest profile: Hinds and Harris 2025 populations 211,888 and 5,045,026; age 65+ 19.4% and 13.1%; Black-alone 70.4% and 20.5%; Hispanic/Latino of any race 3.0% and 46.1%; 2020–2024 median household incomes $49,402 and $74,983; poverty 22.2% and 16.7%; under-65 uninsured 13.3% and 22.2%. These descriptors have differing reference years from cancer rates and overlapping race/ethnicity definitions. Higher Harris uninsured share alongside lower late-stage incidence is a useful challenge to monocausal insurance narratives, not a denial that coverage matters.

QuickFacts source links: https://www.census.gov/quickfacts/fact/table/hindscountymississippi/PST045225 and https://www.census.gov/quickfacts/fact/table/harriscountytexas/PST045225 .

## Counterfactual systems
| Candidate | What changes | Primary valid outcome | What would falsify it |
|---|---|---|---|
| C0: competent status quo | Observe current prevention, screening, diagnosis, policy and population trends | Age-standardized all-stage incidence and cohort trajectories | Unstable definitions, changing ascertainment, misclassified population |
| C1: upstream prevention | Reduce validated risk exposures such as tobacco, harmful alcohol use and modifiable metabolic/activity burden in a locally appropriate way | New invasive cancers over adequate latency, verified exposure changes | Exposures unchanged, incidence trends already parallel elsewhere, opposite mechanisms |
| C2: precancer interception | Expand age/risk-appropriate screening and effective removal of precursor lesions | Incidence after reasonable lag; observed lesions and safe follow-up | Stage migration alone, missed follow-up, screening harm or unequal uptake |
| C3: full prevention and care coordination | Combine upstream prevention, valid screening, closed-loop referral and community choice | New invasive incidence, with equity, safe follow-up, burden, cost and mortality | Capacity shifts, increased harms or costs, no credible improvements after appropriate follow-up |
| C4: transferred intervention | Preserve an empirically supported core function, adapt delivery to a new city | Context-specific measured incidence plus implementation outcomes | Effect modifiers, policy, access, capacity or mechanisms differ; site-specific trial fails |

An intervention could initially increase identified cancer cases through greater testing. A short-term change in screening or case-detection percentage cannot prove prevention. Effect claims require actual treated and comparison populations and time periods, valid instruments, enough observations and consideration of selection, concurrent events and spillover. DDD requires the third subgroup dimension and assumptions; Hinds/Harris cross-sections do not meet this design.

## Integrated evidence architecture and ownership
DIS owns the mission charter, stage and decision ledger, source/denominator verification, Designed–Operational–Experienced comparison, alternative explanations, counterfactual and temporal stewardship. RPLICE owns HIL-selected SALP, CFIR, RE-AIM, Parallax and adaptation methodology through authenticated MCP. People select method and consent to declared data transfer; the output remains provisional unless independently validated. The user-facing GIS sources are NCI Atlas/State Cancer Profiles, CDC PLACES, ACS, USDA Food Environment Atlas and optional ECHO. A source link is not an accepted connected data pipeline.

**Required data in order:** authoritative all-stage invasive incidence by age, sex, race/ethnicity and anatomical subsite with stable historical denominator; late-stage incidence by compatible staging; environmental exposure/behavioral risk with method and uncertainty; real provider screening and follow-up capacity; valid access/experienced-reality studies; matched counterfactual or credible causal design; geographic county/tract boundaries at correct scale; suppression/privacy rules and instrument calibration.

**Phase 1—orientation and validation:** registry scientist, GIS lead, community representative and evaluation scientist align the target definition, 10-year historical series, geographic unit, source quality and measurement. Closure: reproducible tables, harmonized denominators, missingness and uncertainty.
**Phase 2—systems map:** local epidemiologists, public health departments, clinical providers, community representatives and toxicology expertise examine competing sources of risk and protection, service bottlenecks, environmental hypotheses and rival explanations. Closure: evidence-linked, tested hypotheses, no ecological causal overclaims.
**Phase 3—HIL intervention and protocol:** decision owner selects SALP/CFIR/RE-AIM appropriate to the problem, sets core functions versus adaptable delivery and selects ethically appropriate comparisons. Closure: responsible partners, protocol/IRB or QI determination, financing, data-use rights, rehearsal, monitoring and adverse-event rules.
**Phase 4—bounded implementation and evaluation:** implement only through authorized sites; measure observed new invasive cancer cases over appropriate latency alongside screening, polyp removal, referral, health equity, cost, mortality and patient experience. Closure: signed results and independent rival-explanation review.
**Phase 5—translation and scale-out:** compare effect modifiers, resources, capacity and lived experience across jurisdictions, not simply geographic or population resemblance. Plan the day after success, successor responsibilities, financing and decade-long monitoring for changes in cohorts, screening guidance, clinical technologies and exposures.

## System and claims status
The first version of the DIS Population Health Atlas has a typed 15-county colorectal late-stage cohort, large-county screening, source/CI display, case-specific context, Leaflet map and optional EPA ECHO map service. The UI identifies external datasets that are **not** integrated and other diseases that do **not** have accepted incidence observations. It must pass an exact-commit build, original-source recheck, browser/map overlay check and environmental methods review before being represented as production-accepted. No partner has been contacted or clinical care initiated by this deliverable.
