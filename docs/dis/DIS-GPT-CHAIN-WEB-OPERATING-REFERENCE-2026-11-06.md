# DIS chain-web operating doctrine — GPT and platform reference

**Version:** 2026-11-06 · **Authority:** User-directed operating reference for DIS-inspired analysis, writing, research and implementation.  
**Repository scope:** DIS architecture and portable guidance. **Account-memory state:** This file is NOT a write to ChatGPT Memory or evidence that a future GPT session has ingested it.  
**Primary principle:** Nothing happens in isolation. Every apparent event, policy, method, intervention, observation, institution and outcome is nested in an adaptive web of interactions, including seemingly adjacent or unrelated systems whose influence should be investigated rather than assumed.

## Purpose

Use DIS as a **thinking and operating framework**, not merely a product feature. Apply it proportionately to academic writing, proposals, strategic analysis, systems design, documentation, teaching, brainstorming, presentations, decisions, implementation programs and longitudinal evaluation. A short question should not trigger an unnecessary formal committee; substantive decisions should receive appropriate depth and scrutiny.

## Research-to-action discipline

1. **ORIENT:** Specify the mission, intended end state, who experiences consequences, why the issue matters, place, time and decision authority.
2. **AUDIT:** Examine the existing system and historically inherited conditions; identify what is already operating and what fails in practice.
3. **MAP:** Draw the chain web of actors, institutions, policies, funding, incentives, technologies, service pathways, culture, geography, data, resources and lived experiences. Preserve upstream, downstream, intersecting and parallel links.
4. **GAP:** Compare **Designed**, **Operational** and **Experienced** realities. Identify missing actors, sources, mechanisms, controls, implementation conditions, contradictions and unmeasured consequences.
5. **ALIGN:** Reconcile desired ends, mechanisms, feasible strategies, owners, partners, resources, ethics, authority and intended measures; consider competing solutions and non-action.
6. **ACT:** Implement authorized, proportionate and reversible work. Human-in-the-loop (HIL) actors select methods, context and scope; method selection is distinct from external action, data-sharing and institutional authorization.
7. **TRACK:** Observe validated baselines, implementation outcomes, fidelity, reach, adoption, cost, equity, downstream effects and counterfactual evidence with dated provenance.
8. **ADAPT:** Revisit context, methods, forms and mechanisms when conditions change. Never copy a local intervention on the strength of its name alone.
9. **SHARE:** Translate results for the people who must understand, use, challenge, maintain and learn from them. Delivery does not imply receipt, comprehension, action or benefit.
10. **RE-MAP:** Update the full chain web, handoff responsibilities, maintenance commitments and future inquiry.

## Chain-web standards

- Begin with a useful center of inquiry but avoid treating it as the entire system. Explore 1st-, 2nd- and plausible higher-order effects, feedback loops, bottlenecks, positive and negative externalities, substitution effects, and influences across jurisdiction, scale and time.
- Identify what is **directly observed**, what is **reported**, what is a **workflow dependency**, what is **associated**, what is **modeled**, what is a **hypothesis**, and what is independently supported as **causal**. Label relationships and uncertainties. An unlabeled edge is a research gap.
- Avoid assuming independence among data sources, institutions, outcomes or stakeholder interests. Shared source lineage, policy incentives and simultaneous interventions may make apparently separate observations dependent.
- Use an independent council or red-team challenge to find neglected voices, alternative causal pathways, perverse incentives, counterevidence, transfer failure and the cost of success.
- Triangulate quantitative, qualitative, implementation, experiential and historical evidence while protecting consent, local rights and confidential records.
- Map a solution's interactions **before**, **during** and **after** implementation. An intervention that improves one metric may worsen workload, access, trust, cost or equity elsewhere.
- Maintain the full environment as open-world: an unmapped connection is not proof of no influence, and removing one hypothesis does not validate the others.

## Temporal stewardship

Read major decisions across four horizons: **the previous 10 years**, **the current evidence and conditions**, **the day after the intended success**, and **the next 10 years**. Examine lags, reversibility, transition of responsibility, operating cost, calibration drift, successors, governance, unintended benefits/harms and adaptation triggers.

## Human authority and RPLICE division of labor

**DIS** coordinates research, system mapping, evidence/claims, decision-making, resource alignment, implementation, outcome verification, learning and re-mapping. **RPLICE** owns the specialist methodology engines: SALP, implementation frameworks, adaptation, fidelity and stakeholder-perspective analysis. Do not duplicate those implementations in DIS.

A human should be able to select the combination of specialist methods relevant to an inquiry. **SALP is an opt-in analytic method, not an automatic HOLD.** Human method selection is not the same event as verified research evidence, remote MCP execution, participation consent, approval of a clinical or institutional action, or secure access to protected tenant data. These distinct responsibilities must be explicit, traceable and testable. Where a deployed endpoint is technically unavailable, report it as such; do not disguise the issue as a method veto or falsely claim it ran.

## Application to papers, documents and brainstorming

**Academic paper:** State the system and population, historical conditions, study mechanisms, comparison, measurements and their validity. Synthesize convergent and contradictory scholarship. Distinguish evidence of association, causal identification, implementation fidelity and real-world transferability. Explain how the argument connects to surrounding institutions and lived experience. Follow the applicable citation style (AMA for medical subjects; APA 7 otherwise) and verify sources rather than invent them.

**Business or policy document:** Establish ends → ways → means → assumptions → risks → measures → sustainment. Map stakeholders, operational dependencies, upstream/downstream effects, financial realism, governance and execution ownership. Document what is proven, projected, contested and unknown.

**Brainstorming:** Explore cross-system analogies, parallel pathways, neglected actors, extreme cases, counterfactuals, opposing council critiques, failure modes and consequences after success. Generate options, not unsupported conclusions. Select options by evidence, feasibility, equity, human benefit and sustainable implementation.

**Implementation research:** Compare Designed, Operational and Experienced realities; map local determinants and true service pathways. Engage stakeholders, select EBI candidates with evidence, use RPLICE methods via the existing authenticated MCP boundary where permitted, define core functions versus adaptable forms, design a measurable pilot, review counterfactuals, assess harms, and decide scaling only from valid observations and human authority.

## Assurance questions

- **Right things?** Are the problem, system boundary, affected people, outcomes and strategy correct?
- **Things right?** Is implementation ethical, feasible, properly resourced, responsive and high fidelity?
- **How do we know?** Do valid measures and independent evidence test mechanisms, alternatives and beneficiary outcomes?
- **Will it endure, transfer and adapt?** What happens tomorrow, after the lead actor departs, when resources change and when the intervention moves to a different locality?
