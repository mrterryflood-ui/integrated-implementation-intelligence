# DIS × RPLICE: Double-Helix Research-to-Action Architecture
**Version:** 0.1, research hypothesis; **date:** 2026-11-07  
**Status:** Internal prior-art reconnaissance, not a systematic review, priority claim, validated instrument, or external publication.  
**Method:** SciSpace indexed abstract/metadata search; original articles, DOI, peer-review status, complete methods and Google Scholar indexing **not yet independently verified**. References below are *discovery leads*, not independently validated evidence.

## Scientific claim and falsifiable proposition
The proposed architecture intertwines two versioned, bidirectional but non-substitutable chains:
1. **Epistemic strand:** phenomenon → construct → instrument and numerator/denominator → provenance → uncertainty and bias → rival explanation → discriminating design → independently reviewed inference.
2. **Implementation strand:** intended benefit and equity → institutional capability → human experience and consent → strategy/core function → authorized action → reach/fidelity/cost/harms → independently observed outcome → adaptation and sustainment.

**Coupling rule:** no actionable claim may inherit stronger epistemic status than its weakest required evidence link; no implementation success may be inferred from a delivered output or self-reported receipt. Cross-strand feedback is a versioned, attributable event, not an automatic causal conclusion.

**Potential incremental contribution (hypothesis):** a single typed, testable decision chain that prevents denominator/construct conflation, requires adversarial explanations and explicit method selection, ties evidence to institutional execution and lived experience, and tracks both epistemic and implementation uncertainty over time. Each component has substantial prior art. Novelty must be evaluated in the *integrated, empirically superior behavior*, not in claiming any component is new.

## Prior-art reconnaissance: compare rather than displace
| Related body of work | What it already contributes | Distinguishing question for DIS–RPLICE |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Learning Health Systems + D&I science | Cyclic evidence generation, application, adaptation, engagement and system infrastructure | Can DIS trace and enforce measurement validity and independently verified correction across both strands better than an existing mature LHS? |
| Evidence-to-decision (GRADE/Navigation Guide) | Transparent benefit/harm, certainty, equity, feasibility and recommendation criteria | Can a machine-checkable evidence-action contract reduce unsupported inference without excluding stakeholder judgment? |
| Public health evidence knowledge graphs | Structured representation of source relationships and decision evidence | Does DIS add source-specific denominator, inference, governance and longitudinal action verification beyond graph traversal? |
| Modern DID and sensitivity analysis | Formal counterfactual identification, pretrend limitations, robustness to deviations | Does DIS route the right study to the right method, rather than treat metadata as a causal validity certificate? |
| Implementation frameworks (CFIR, RE-AIM, PRISM, FRAME-IS) | Context, determinants, reach, effectiveness, adaptation, maintenance | Does orchestration demonstrably improve decision quality and follow-through without replacing the specialist frameworks? |

## Proposed preregistered validation, not completed
**Unit:** bounded research-to-action cases, beginning with colorectal registry comparisons. **Comparators:** conventional epidemiologist/implementation scientist workflow, existing evidence-to-decision + LHS process, DIS without RPLICE method selection, and full DIS–RPLICE. Use identical input packets and independent blinded adjudicators where feasible.

**Primary outcomes:** percentage of invalid denominator/construct substitutions prevented; unsupported causal claims per case; proportion of evidence-action links with source and reviewer lineage; time-to-valid recommendation; independently verified intervention completion and outcome; and inter-rater agreement on claim validity.

**Equity and balancing outcomes:** false reassurance in suppressed or underrepresented strata; burden on community/clinical participants; accessibility of explanations; subgroup measurement validity; time/cost/energy; number of decisions deferred or escalated; unintended harm. Report denominator and missingness for every metric.

**Reproducibility:** freeze inputs, schemas, source snapshots, version hashes, analysis code, scenario seed and expected decisions. Independent reproduction must include failure cases, blinded review and negative controls; engineering test success alone does not establish scientific performance.

**Red-team challenge:** a strong rival could reproduce most gains with a well-configured LHS, standard evidence-to-decision checklist, provenance-aware graph, and expert human review. We must test that rival directly.
