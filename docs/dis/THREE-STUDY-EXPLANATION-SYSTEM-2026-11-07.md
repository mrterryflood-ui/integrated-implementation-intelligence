# DIS | Three-study recurring explanation system
**Internal scientific design, 2026-11-07.** No external correspondence or clinical action. This is a research protocol and decision contract, not proof of causal mechanisms.

## Mission
Explain recurring differences between disease occurrence, observation, diagnosis, delivery and lived experience. Prevent numerical resemblance from being mistaken for causal equivalence, and prevent intervention fidelity from being mistaken for intervention appropriateness. Preserve a consistent cross-study reasoning process while allowing each disease its own valid measurement model.

## Unified observation-to-explanation model
Define **latent phenomenon** (true occurrence and severity), **eligibility/exposure** (population at risk and selection), **contact** (ability to reach care), **recognition** (screening, symptoms, test sensitivity, diagnostic thresholds), **completion** (referral and diagnostic closure), **recording** (registry/coding, stage and missingness), **experienced consequence** (symptoms, burden, trust, harm), and **downstream outcome** (incidence, severity, mortality, function, quality of life). These are related but not interchangeable. Draw a causal DAG per condition before choosing an adjustment set. Selection into the diagnosed population can distort associations; do not assume a simple multiplicative identity or derive disease prevalence from ever-diagnosed survey rates.

Every result is a tuple: condition, construct, numerator, denominator, eligibility frame, geography/boundary, period, age/sex/race definition, method/staging criteria, standardization, uncertainty, source version, retrieval time, suppression/missingness, provenance and verification state. Comparison is **REJECTED**, **CONDITIONALLY_DESCRIPTIVE**, or **SOURCE_HARMONIZED**. Harmonized does not mean causal.

## Study 1 — Hinds vs Harris colorectal, Colorado extension
**Observed source-reported historical contrast:** 2018–2022 Hinds late-stage share 63.3%, age-adjusted late-stage incidence 30.4/100k (95% CI 27.3–33.8), mean late-stage count 76/year. Harris share 63.4%, rate 23.3/100k (95% CI 22.7–24.0), count 1,044/year. Rates differ by 7.1 per 100k (Hinds/Harris ratio approximately 1.305); the conditional shares differ by 0.1 percentage point. These are not Jackson/Houston city measures. The reported 95% CIs for late-stage incidence do not overlap, but do not substitute that visual fact for a specified inferential test or population-level causal explanation.
**What this demonstrates:** two conditional stage shares can be similar while population-based late-stage incidence differs. Stage share, rate and count share source lineage and are not independent replications.
**Competing hypotheses:** (a) underlying all-stage disease occurrence differs; (b) screening and early detection shift observed stage distribution; (c) abnormal-test resolution and diagnostic capacity differ; (d) stage coding and unstaged fractions differ; (e) risk, migration, case mix, socioeconomic context or access differ. The source table alone cannot discriminate them.
**Required investigation:** source-pinned matched-year all-stage and late-stage rates, all-stage counts and stage completeness; standardization and confidence intervals; time series and subgroup disaggregation; screening-eligible and abnormal-result cohorts, dated invitation, completion and resolution; catchment and contextual policy; independent source adjudication. Do not algebraically derive all-stage age-adjusted incidence by dividing late-stage rate by late-stage percentage. Texas all-stage and late-stage official queries currently show different reporting vintages, and Colorado has 2019–2023 versus the Hinds/Harris 2018–2022 baseline; retain nonpoolable status.
**Mechanism discriminator:** if all-stage incidence is elevated with similar stage distribution, investigate disease occurrence and exposure mechanisms; if all-stage rates are similar but late-stage incidence or diagnostic delays diverge, investigate pathway and classification mechanisms. These are proposed conditional tests, not established facts.
**Decision gate:** no causal estimate from a five-year cross-sectional summary; no intervention until mechanism and intended benefit are separately specified.

## Study 2 — fifty-state colorectal, lung and female breast cancer
**Observed in the prior pinned repository table:** Pearson colorectal–lung late-stage incidence correlation approximately 0.735, versus late-stage share correlation approximately 0.129. These are ecological descriptive associations; sample definition is 50 states, not patients. The prior dossier records unresolved secondary correlation discrepancies: colorectal–breast late-stage share Pearson 0.234 previously vs 0.230670 recomputed; lung–breast share Spearman 0.413 vs 0.409816. Reconcile exact input revision, rounding, missingness and algorithm before reporting a final table.
**Scientific explanation to test:** incidence rates use a population-based, age-adjusted denominator, while stage shares condition on diagnosed cases. A common population risk structure may correlate with rates even when the stage distribution among diagnosed cases does not. Conversely, screening intensity, stage completeness and case mix may alter shares. This is a plausible explanation, not demonstrated by correlations.
**Tests:** rerun pinned source CSV/script with hashes; compare Pearson and Spearman, rank and leave-one-state-out sensitivity, influential observations, age/sex stratification, nonindependence, missingness and uncertainty; use site-specific stage definitions and avoid interpreting cancer types as exchangeable. Evaluate underlying all-stage incidence, mortality, screening access and relevant exposures in separate aligned panels. Cross-state correlations cannot establish individual risks, shared causes, screening efficacy or program effectiveness.
**Decision gate:** reproduce all coefficients exactly and harmonize original source vintage before any causal narrative. Where values conflict, publish a discrepancy ledger rather than selecting the more convenient estimate.

## Study 3 — gynecologic diagnostic ascertainment
**Verified original national report:** NCHS Health E-Stat 121 (Nugent and Lochner, July 2026), 2022–2023 NSFG, 4,856 women aged 20–49; ever-diagnosed endometriosis 6.2% (95% CI 5.2–7.2), PCOS 8.7% (7.7–9.8), fibroids 7.8% (6.8–8.9). The survey asks whether respondents have *ever been diagnosed*, not whether they currently have underlying disease, what caused it, or when onset occurred.
**Specific contrasts:** endometriosis ever-diagnosed: non-Hispanic White 8.1%, non-Hispanic Black 3.7%, lower family income <150% FPL 4.4%, ≥300% FPL 7.1%. PCOS: non-Hispanic Black 4.4% vs non-Hispanic White 9.9%. Fibroids: non-Hispanic Black 12.5% vs non-Hispanic White 7.1%; privately insured 8.8% vs uninsured 5.2%. These are bivariate survey estimates with varying CIs and do not isolate race, income, insurance, clinical access or biological cause. Fibroid diagnosis is strongly age-patterned (20–29: 1.4%, 40–49: 15.8%). Do not infer disease absence from non-diagnosis or infer an individual diagnosis.
**Rival explanations:** true underlying condition frequency, symptom recognition, thresholds and clinician practice, healthcare contact, specialist/referral capacity, insurance, stigma, recall and survey selection, age distribution. Distinguish condition-specific pathways: endometriosis symptoms/diagnostic delay, PCOS diagnostic criteria and clinical recognition, fibroid symptom/imaging ascertainment and treatment. A higher ever-diagnosed percentage may reflect higher disease burden, better detection, or both.
**Tests:** reproduce complex-sample weighted estimates and CIs from NSFG public-use data using documented design variables; review diagnostic question wording and denominator; model age and other prespecified covariates where permitted; assess recall, access and symptom experience using independently authorized data. The national survey is not a state-by-state prevalence panel. Do not equate NSFG survey diagnosis estimates with cancer registry stage measures.
**Primary source:** https://www.cdc.gov/nchs/data/hestat/hestat121.htm

## Reusable research workflow: EXPLAIN
**E — Establish the construct:** What phenomenon, numerator, denominator, unit, geography, time, and intended beneficiary are we actually measuring? Reject ambiguous labels.
**X — eXamine observation pathways:** Map latent occurrence → recognition → diagnostic completion → recording → lived consequences; distinguish designed, operational and experienced realities.
**P — Propose rivals:** Minimum three plausible competing mechanisms, each with disconfirming evidence and explicit unknowns; prevent the favored theory from monopolizing explanation.
**L — Link evidence:** Pin source version and lineage, harmonize populations/periods/uncertainty, identify duplicate evidence, missingness, ecological fallacy, selection and measurement artifacts.
**A — Assess discrimination and action:** Which evidence would change our decision? Select appropriate descriptive, quasi-experimental or prospective methods; specify plausible intervention, alternatives, harms, costs and owner. A DID screen is not a causal estimate.
**I — Independently challenge:** Red-team scientific and lived-experience assumptions, validate instruments, inspect adverse subgroup effects, document dissent and source contradictions.
**N — Navigate follow-through:** Authorize only after human review; capture case history, baseline, delivery, fidelity, beneficiary benefit and harm, sustainment and reopen triggers.

## Minimum system contract
Inputs: versioned source rows, construct ontology, population crosswalk, hypothesis ledger, methods/data-quality notes, evidence and decision receipts. Outputs per study: (1) what is observed; (2) what cannot be inferred; (3) ranked *test priorities* (not fabricated probability of causes); (4) competing mechanisms and discriminator; (5) source/method gaps; (6) next admissible analysis; (7) decision and risk gate; (8) owner and closure proof; (9) accessible plain-language explanation with a parallel technical trace.
Status states: DISCOVERED → SOURCE_VERIFIED → HARMONIZED → DESCRIPTIVE_EXPLAINED → MECHANISM_TESTED → DESIGN_REVIEWED → ACTION_AUTHORIZED → OUTCOME_WITNESSED → SUSTAINED. These are **separate evidence gates**; no automatic promotion from correlation, metadata search, receipt, synthetic test or successful CI. A scientific finding can be contradicted/reopened without deleting history.
Interfaces: existing DIS Research Studio + private case history + human-selected RPLICE specialist; reuse GitHub as authoritative code, no new vendor or database by default. A machine-readable contract and automated tests should implement the same gates; do not confuse this document with deployed functionality.
Metrics: correct construct classification, source-lineage traceability, contradictions caught, false causal conclusions prevented, discriminating-test usefulness, inter-rater agreement, explanation comprehension, time to evidence resolution, subgroup harm detection, and verified decision change; report burden and costs. Compare with competent existing epidemiology/implementation-science workflow to establish incremental value.
