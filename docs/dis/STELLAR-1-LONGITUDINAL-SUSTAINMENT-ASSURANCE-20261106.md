# DIS STELLAR 1 → Implementation → Day-After-Success

**Research-stage integration release candidate** | 2026-11-06  
**Scope:** Public source-qualified Jackson/Hinds cancer case, destination-specific Houston transportability checks, bounded generic cases, and read-only DIS MCP research blueprints. No real clinical or institutional execution.

## Governing doctrine: the car that was caught

The mission is **not** to achieve a milestone and then lose the capability that made it worthwhile. A "successful" intervention may expand demand, shift the bottleneck downstream, change the observed population, divert scarce resources, create new inequities, alter incentives or require a successor when the champion leaves. DIS must ask what happens on the day after success as seriously as what to do if a pilot fails.

STELLAR 1 begins with **what is being done, for whom, to understand or deliver what, where, for how long, to change what, and compared with which competent existing reality**. It explicitly includes who benefits and bears risk, historical roots, local demography, alternative perspectives, center of the problem, adjacent systems, upstream/downstream dependencies, intersections and parallel processes. A graph distance is only a *declared relationship-hop count* until supported by original evidence; it never automatically becomes measured causal influence.

## Scenario planning — generated but not observed

Each research project now has five compulsory outcome rehearsals.

| Branch | Required inquiry | Authorized operational implication |
|---|---|---|
| NOT_TESTED | What is unknown before a first pilot? What tests distinguish alternatives? | Define lawful research, roles and baseline; no actual intervention |
| FAILURE | Was the failure of the EBI mechanism, instrument, implementation, context or incorrect goal? | Preserve established services; re-map, revise, reverse or retire through human authority |
| PARTIAL_SUCCESS | Who benefited? Who was excluded? Did only a proxy move? | Analyze fidelity, distribution, inequality, missingness, subgroup error and costs |
| SUCCESS | Can staffing, budget, capacity, data, clinical safety and equity endure when pilot support ends? | Transition to an accountable successor, resource and continuity agreement; **no automatic scale-up** |
| UNINTENDED_HARM | Did the action create displaced capacity, unsafe decisions, exclusion, measurement manipulation or privacy harm? | Follow authorized incident and safety protocol; pause/reverse the risky intervention where authorized, not essential care |

Every branch contains leading questions, alternative explanations, early indicators, testable counteractions, proof requirements and actual human roles. All outputs remain hypothetical until a separately verified outcome and corresponding authority are recorded.

## Dynamic learning and sustainment

STELLAR is the first gate; the existing DIS workflow then spans orientation, sources, population and measures, causal challenge, co-design, SALP, EBI mechanism fit, SMART logic, governance, planning, pilot, authorized delivery, monitoring, evaluation, adaptation, communications, translation, cross-site transfer and sustainment. The final phase feeds *back into a new STELLAR assessment* when evidence, values, institutions, demographics, instruments or core mechanisms change.

Temporal triggers are specified for the day after apparent success, early operational cycles, repeated monthly reviews, the outcome-relevant longer term, and future successors. The exact calendar, thresholds and procedures depend on the scientific and operational domain and must be adopted by the appropriate owner—no universal 30/60/90-day guarantee is assumed.

## Core function versus adaptable form

The scientific distinction is between the hypothesized causal **core function** and its delivery **form** (communications channel, staff workflow, language, timing, venue, resources, interface). A core function is not assumed true simply because it was declared. Any change should identify what changed, who authorized it, intended mechanism, why the change is necessary, what can harm, and the evidence that will test whether the core function or goal remains valid.

This derives from the principles of FRAME and adaptation research: changes must be described by timing, target, source, purpose and mechanism, and their effects on implementation and beneficiary outcomes empirically evaluated.¹⁻³

## The evidence cannot support automatic site replication

The 2026 Cochrane review of tailored interventions to address determinants of professional practice found a modest average effect of tailored versus non-tailored strategies (OR 1.49; 95% CI 1.26–1.75; 23 pooled studies) with substantial heterogeneity (I² 82%). That is **not evidence that any one EBI will work in Jackson or Houston**. Tailoring did not uniformly benefit all settings; mechanisms and overall cost-effectiveness remain unsettled.⁴

A 2025 randomized trial of patient navigation after abnormal stool cancer screening demonstrated improved *follow-up completion*. That process endpoint cannot, by itself, establish a reduction in population late-stage cancer incidence, morbidity or mortality.⁵ The cancer-stage literature warns that the percentage of detected cases diagnosed late can improve because many more early cancers are found even when late-stage disease burden is unchanged. We therefore use population-based, age-adjusted late-stage incidence and mortality as distal metrics, alongside legitimate implementation and service measures.⁶

The Implementation Research Logic Model (IRLM) links **determinants → implementation strategies → hypothesized mechanisms → implementation, service and individual outcomes**, and recognizes adaptation, timing, add-on strategies and displacement. DIS reuses this logic rather than claiming to invent implementation science.⁷

Context transportability requires appropriate source-to-destination effect modifiers, overlap of eligible populations, compatible instruments and actual outcome verification—not merely similar demographics, labels or proximity.⁸

## RPLICE / DIS MCP responsibility boundary

- **DIS owns:** initial mission/STELLAR analysis, human/user scope and consent, source provenance and challenge, cross-domain research orchestration, standardized handoff, visual implementation plan, evidence ledger, human approval and longitudinal reassessment.
- **RPLICE owns:** its specialist Structural Anchor and Leverage Protocol (SALP), established implementation methods, Parallax and analytical routines, through a separately verified and permissioned method contract.
- **The DIS MCP** provides two new **read-only** tools: `dis_stellar_one` and `dis_research_implementation_blueprint`. The latter returns a source-qualified draft case, 20 stage-gated phases, SMART logic and scenario rehearsals, site transportability, and a **NOT SENT** RPLICE method-handoff proposal.
- **Actual RPLICE execution:** not enabled or inferred from the public tool. A catalogue listing or available URL is not a completed partner method, durable checkpoint or verified user decision.

RPLICE's authoritative tenant, authorization, application/credential and live-tool contracts must be confirmed separately before any private method call, record transfer, or claimed implementation.
