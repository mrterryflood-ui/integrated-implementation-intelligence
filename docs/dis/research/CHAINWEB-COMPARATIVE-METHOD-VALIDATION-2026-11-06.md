# DIS–RPLICE ChainWeb: Comparative Methods Validation, Adaptation and Sustainment
**Protocol and evidence preflight v0.1 | November 6, 2026 | Research draft; not registered or independently approved**

## Executive decision
**Main question:** Does adding ChainWeb's explicit, versioned evidence-to-decision-to-correction chain to competent existing implementation-science practice improve identification of material implementation problems, context-sensitive corrective decisions, fidelity preservation, and long-term sustainment **without increasing unsupported causal conclusions, staff burden, cost, or inequity**?

**Study classification:** An implementation-science *methods evaluation*. Existing evidence-based clinical programs are benchmark cases, not interventions already delivered by DIS. The **research intervention under evaluation is ChainWeb-supported decision-making**, not mailed FIT, patient navigation, or cancer treatment. The current casebook and deterministic tests establish a safe, traceable **evaluation substrate**, not evidence of ChainWeb superiority. An actual comparative study requires independently recruited participants, protected test cases, a pre-registered protocol, outcomes and analysis, independent adjudicators, and governing approval as applicable.

**Decision today:** GO for a version-frozen reproducible casebook and blinded comparative validation preparation; HOLD for any statement of demonstrated superiority or clinical outcome impact. Do not send to Jeremiah or any institution without user review and approval.

## What ChainWeb is — and is not
ChainWeb is a **longitudinal, evidence-linked, multilevel implementation-decision network**. Every claimed connection records an original evidence source, unit/population/time, causal status, competing hypothesis, discriminating test, implementation owner, core intervention function, permitted adaptation, fidelity measure, downstream outcomes, and recheck trigger. Nodes include patients, organizations, services, resources, policy, biology, environment, social conditions, and outcomes, subject to each study's legitimate boundaries. A graph edge is never automatically a demonstrated causal effect. DIS governs MAP–GAP and implementation work; human-directed RPLICE methods support the specific scientific questions where authorized.

A static system map, a list of risk factors, a software build, a favorable source-based correlation, or a simulated Council critique is **not** proof of implementation effectiveness. Selection of an RPLICE method is not evidence of its execution. No protected records or real clinical actions were used in this phase.

## Explicit separation of the three studies
**Study A, current primary:** Test ChainWeb-supported implementation decisions versus a fair contemporary comparator. Primary outcomes are correct critical problem identification, erroneous causal attribution, and appropriate context-specific action selection. Follow through to adaptation and sustainment when implementation resources and time permit.

**Study B, preserved:** Six-condition cancer and women's-health epidemiologic/ascertainment research (colorectal cancer, lung cancer, female breast cancer, endometriosis, PCOS, uterine fibroids). Retain published 50-state cancer and NSFG observations, source uncertainty, the Hinds/Harris/Colorado comparisons, and missing longitudinal state women's-health data; do not infer cross-condition causal effects or merge unlike denominators.

**Study C, explicitly deferred:** Transfer validated knowledge and measurement logic to existing healthcare platforms to identify state-specific equity needs and improve patient- and program-facing services. Only promote independently validated evidence, authorized data flows, institutionally approved implementations, and locally proven adaptations. The intended consumer platforms include HerHealth, Thriving Communities, Mission Transition, and related owned tools; do not automatically repurpose their private data, create cross-tenant access, or copy entire datasets.

## Research hypothesis and genuine scientific alternative
H1: Under equal evidence, time, training and expert support, ChainWeb supplementation increases the proportion of **material, source-supported implementation failure mechanisms** identified compared with competent iterative PRISM–RE-AIM/CFIR/FRAME-IS use alone.

H2: ChainWeb supplementation increases **appropriate, testable, resource-feasible and fidelity-preserving adaptations**, especially when multiple interacting systems or delivery constraints change.

H3: In prospective external settings, ChainWeb supplementation improves **timely detection and correction of implementation deterioration** while maintaining/recovering prespecified RE-AIM outcomes, safety, equity, and affordable sustainment.

**Scientific alternatives:** The comparator performs as well as or better than ChainWeb; ChainWeb adds burden without outcome gains; source and measurement effects explain the apparent advantage; familiarity or additional analyst time rather than the method explains differences; an advantage occurs only in one condition or site and does not transfer. All are acceptable possible study results.

## Best available comparator, not a weak straw man
Comparator (A): Qualified analysts use the **2022 updated CFIR** for determinants, **iterative PRISM–RE-AIM** for context, monitoring, maintenance and action planning, and **FRAME-IS** to document strategy adaptations. They may use standard causal diagrams and eligible statistical tools and should receive the same full case facts, access duration, domain references, and AI assistance where AI is part of both arms.

Experimental supplementation (B): All of A **plus** ChainWeb's explicitly versioned nodes/edges and evidence classifications, upstream/downstream coupling, unresolved contradictions, owner/action/verification chain, and temporal feedback/re-mapping. RPLICE SALP and Parallax are selected only by a human with separate legitimate execution authority; they are not automatic additions and must be documented as actually run or unexecuted.

**Important novelty limit:** Iterative PRISM–RE-AIM already assesses context and adaptation and supports ongoing sustainment; FRAME-IS already documents modifications. ChainWeb must demonstrate an **incremental, measurable advantage** beyond these methods. Recent research explicitly notes difficulties measuring cascading, nested adaptations, offering a useful hypothesis, not evidence of ChainWeb superiority. [1–6]

A 2025 methodological recommendations paper specifically addresses how to estimate the impact of adaptations on implementation outcomes, so our evaluation must use established causal methods rather than claim they are absent. A 2026 scoping review of implementation frameworks used in quality improvement reported that only a minority of reviewed evaluations tested construct relationships or formulated testable hypotheses, but those descriptive review counts are **not** evidence that ChainWeb works better. [14,15]

## Evidence-based interventions and settings actually represented
- **North Carolina FQHC mailed FIT plus navigation (2024 RCT, n=4002):** 6-month screening 30.0% versus 9.7% usual care (adjusted difference 20.29 percentage points; 95% CI, 17.85–22.73). Positive-FIT colonoscopy follow-up contrast had CI crossing zero: this secondary endpoint is not conclusive. Central operations, provider capacity and positive-FIT services are important setting conditions. [7]
- **Multisystem EHR reminders/outreach ± navigation (2023 cluster RCT, n=11,980):** 120-day completed follow-up 31.4% with navigation/outreach versus 22.9% usual care (adjusted difference 8.5; 95% CI, 4.8–12.0). The incremental contrast of navigation *over active outreach* was 0.4 (95% CI, −3.6 to 4.2), not a demonstrated benefit. [8]
- **Washington State post-abnormal-FIT navigation (2025 RCT, n=967 analyzed):** 1-year colonoscopy completion 55.1% versus 42.1%; risk difference 13.0 percentage points (95% CI, 6.5–19.4). The outcome is diagnostic completion, not reduced incidence or mortality. [9]
- **Rural Oregon Medicaid screening program (2025 cluster RCT, n=5614):** 6-month screening 11.8% versus 4.5%; adjusted difference 7.3 (95% CI, 5.3–9.2). Mailed kits reached 100% of the 1,489 patients remaining after eligibility scrutiny, while documented navigation reached 11/19 people with abnormal FIT (57.9%). The conditional denominators are not interchangeable. [10]
- **Southern California sustainment challenge (2026 cluster RCT, 16 clinics):** comparison advantage 10.8 (95% CI, 4.9–16.7) at year 2, narrowing to 2.9 (95% CI, −3.2 to 9.0) at year 3. Intervention-group performance did not decline from 37.8% to 39.4%, while usual care increased from 27.0% to 36.5%. Annual eligible cohorts and timing changed. These aggregate observations do **not** establish whether delivery drift, more effective usual care or compositional change caused the result. [11]
- **AltaMed California FQHC (2025 observational program):** FIT return among navigation recipients increased from 36.6% (2021) to 51.0% (2023), but no random assigned counterfactual established a causal navigation effect. Annual program costs and colonoscopy completion gaps inform implementation and sustainment constraints. [12]
- **PCOS diagnostic underrecognition (2024 publication):** observational context, not a randomized intervention. Used as a *negative control* against recommending clinical implementation from group differences alone. [13]
- **FRAME-IS adaptation case (2026 methods application; 32 clinics):** demonstrates that established frameworks can capture adaptations yet encounter complexity with cascading and nested changes. Not a head-to-head ChainWeb test. [6]

The code package includes the full source-linked casebook, original reported point estimates and CIs, explicit missing-data boundaries, and exact source identifiers. It does not pool unlike outcomes or place implementation results on an incidence scale.

## Immediate completed methods preflight
Eight published evidence cases and **nine** distinct reported numerical contrasts were transcribed with sources, populations, timepoints, comparator and separate intervention/core-function definitions. A deterministic validator checks distinct case identifiers, study design, required sources, endpoint/timepoint/denominator annotations, consistency of reported rounded contrasts, valid 95% intervals and properly conditional fidelity denominators. Adversarial tests deliberately reject (a) a false claim that navigation's incremental benefit was established where CI crosses zero; (b) a false claim of no effect where year-3 CI crosses zero; (c) rebranding a nonrandomized year-to-year change as causal effectiveness; (d) transferring observational PCOS evidence into clinical practice automatically; and (e) claiming ChainWeb's superiority from other investigators' clinical outcomes. **This is engineering/evidence boundary preflight, not human-blinded method comparison.**

## Comparative evaluation to run next: exact research procedure
1. **Freeze casebook training exemplars** and develop a disjoint, held-out case set with independent source review. At least one null/negative case, one strongly positive trial, one competing treatment component, one sustainment issue, and one multilevel adaptation case. Do not train or tune the experimental approach on held-out cases. Label observational versus randomized cases and preserve contradictions.
2. **Independent adjudication panel:** Epidemiologist, implementation scientist, biostatistician, domain clinician, patient/community representative and operational/sustainment lead. Each provides independently signed rationale and cites original studies; adjudicators are not asked to endorse ChainWeb. Set adjudication rules and resolve disagreements without erasing minority views.
3. **Randomization and parity:** Randomly allocate competent analyst teams or repeated cases to A or B using a balanced crossover or cluster design appropriate to experience/contamination. Equal evidence, available time, citation tools and basic assistance. Analysts using ChainWeb cannot be given extra study facts or unbounded additional time. Blind graders to arm and remove ChainWeb branding from outputs.
4. **Core decision task:** State the actual implementation problem; distinguish clinical EBI outcome from implementation/fidelity/ascertainment; identify source-supported mechanisms and rivals; choose an EBI/implementation strategy or defer; specify delivery site fit, owners/resources, core-vs-form distinction, ethics, equity, action and stop criteria; propose measures, denominators, timepoints and review triggers.
5. **Primary blinded outcome:** Rate of correctly identified adjudicated *material failure mechanisms* per case with documentation of false or unsupported causal claims. Both sensitivity and precision matter; cases with no established mechanisms should reward the ability to say 'unknown.'
6. **Key secondary outcomes:** Correct intervention/context fit; inappropriate overreach; correct identification of fidelity drift versus changing comparator/eligibility; quality of adaptation decisions; decisional time and analyst effort; completeness of core-function preservation, communication and human decision authority; interpretability; equity/safety considerations; projected costs; calibrated uncertainty. Evaluate stakeholder-experienced dimensions with actual participating raters, not synthetic personas.
7. **Statistical design:** Preregister estimands, sample size and meaningful effect margins after a *blinded* feasibility/pilot variance assessment. Use reviewer/case-aware mixed-effects models or a well-defined randomization-based approach, account for clustered or repeated ratings, and report uncertainty/multiplicity. A user-authored deterministic score is not an independent clinical gold standard. Do not designate A inferior before the study.
8. **Falsification:** Deliberately provide cases where a narrow indicator is correct; add misleading correlations, denominator shifts, negative/conflicting data, simulated instrument drift, cost and workforce changes, and context shifts. Measure false alarms, recommendation reversibility and excess burden. Restore cases' real sourced context during human adjudication.
9. **Prospective delivery phase, only after comparative value and partners:** A local authorized site could implement a selected evidence-based clinical intervention under actual quality, ethics, privacy and service authority, then evaluate whether ChainWeb makes implementation decisions more reliable than A. Use appropriate cluster, stepped-wedge, interrupted time-series or adaptive design dictated by the question—not automatic DDD. Monitor patient safety and avoid withholding guideline-standard care.
10. **Temporal follow-through:** Baseline, implementation, follow-up and sustainment windows matched to outcome dynamics (e.g., months/years rather than arbitrary universal 90-day success). Document day-after-success successor, budget, maintenance ownership and re-evaluation criteria. A positive short-term decision score is not proof of sustained patient benefit.

## Fidelity and sustainability measurement contract
**Clinical EBI core functions** are specified from each original trial and relevant current guidelines, not invented by DIS. **Delivery forms** may vary where local implementation and evidence permit. Use FRAME-IS to classify adaptations as proactive/reactive, affected component, reason, decision-maker, timing and preserved core. For every site and period record:
- **Reach and denominator:** clinically eligible people correctly identified and reached; reason for exclusions; subgroup representativeness;
- **Adherence, dose, timing and quality:** what was delivered to whom, when, by whom, with appropriate competence, and what was deferred because already completed;
- **Diagnostic closure:** abnormal tests requiring action, disposition completed within a clinically specified interval, missing records, and appropriate escalation;
- **Organizational adoption/capacity:** staff and partner participation, workflow burden, turnover, appointment/diagnostic supply and training;
- **Adaptation and drift:** core-function deviations versus legitimate local form changes, source of decision, approval, prior and follow-up measures;
- **Cost and equity:** marginal cost, staffing burden, accessibility, distribution of benefit and potential unintended harm;
- **Maintenance:** benefit and quality across repeat cycles, financing, successor handoff, ongoing feedback, cessation/restart policy.

**No automated clinical recommendation or private data egress follows from this protocol.** A fidelity percentage with the wrong denominator, an intervention name without a workflow, a recommendation without an implementation owner or a causal finding without valid identification remains unacceptable.

## Governance and independent proof gates
- **G0 Evidence integrity:** validate the original studies; freeze and checksum the public casebook and source manifest; check RCT protocol deviations and citation accuracy; update with corrections if original-source changes are found.
- **G1 Engineering preflight:** reproducible local code/tests, input bounds, reject invalid study/outcome comparisons, refuse unearned superiority/deployment claims. **Completed for this v0.1 casebook, not a product release.**
- **G2 Scientific adjudication:** independent trained raters, independent holdout cases, transparent scoring rubric, inter-rater agreement, conflict record and preregistered outcomes. **Not completed.**
- **G3 Comparative method evidence:** same evidence, qualified comparator, randomization, analytic receipt, significant/meaningful improvement and no safety/effort regression, or document null/negative. **Not completed.**
- **G4 Site implementation:** real site sponsor, local context/eligibility/clinical capacity, IRB/QI/data rights, staffing, comparator and fidelity measurement. **Not completed.**
- **G5 Sustainment and transfer:** observed longitudinal benefit, cost, equity, adaptations and successful external validation, with willingness to reject unsupported transports. **Not completed.**

At each gate record owner, source, original observation, method, decision, reviewer, date and remaining uncertainty. GO/HOLD pertains to that gate, never automatically to another. Clinical and institutional approval cannot be inferred from ownership of the DIS software.
