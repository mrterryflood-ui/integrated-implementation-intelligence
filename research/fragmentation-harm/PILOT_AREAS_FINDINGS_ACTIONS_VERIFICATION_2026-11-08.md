# ChainWeb pilot-area findings → justified changes → verification plan (2026-11-08)

**Decision status:** actionable research/quality-improvement recommendations, not an institutional implementation authorization or demonstrated DIS–RPLICE patient benefit. Prepared for reconciler, judge and plain-language stakeholder translation. Uses the original 2019–2023 public aggregate snapshot, published trials, and source-bound limitations. DIS/RPLICE are the core; HerHealth and mental-health platforms are supporting contexts. Dartmouth accelerator is separate.

## Bottom line for the two calibration counties

**Hinds County, MS and Harris County, TX (2019–2023):** all-stage age-adjusted colorectal cancer incidence **46.4 vs 38.0 per 100,000** (Hinds +8.4, about22.1% relative); late-stage incidence **29.9 vs23.9 per 100,000** (Hinds +6.0, about25.1% relative). Case-based late-stage proportions **63.7% vs63.4%**, a **0.3-percentage-point** difference. A nearly identical case-based proportion **does not imply equal population burden**. Rates and case proportions have different denominators. The age-adjusted late/all-stage quotients are **not** case-based shares and cannot replace them.

**Meaning:** Hinds' recorded late-stage population burden is materially higher in the retained public data despite a similar late-stage share among diagnosed cases. **Not established:** the difference is caused by any specific FQHC, clinician, standard, insurer, referral network, missed symptom or social determinant; nor that an intervention will move late diagnoses below early diagnoses. Published data do not identify local individual-level first signals, decisions, care completion or causes. The 336-county exploratory stress test demonstrates selective rural missingness and sensitivity to small-count filtering; it does not independently replicate a causal mechanism.

## Condition-specific first changes

**CRC:** separate symptomatic evaluation, hereditary surveillance, and average-risk screening. Do not apply age45/50 screening rules as vetoes on symptom investigation. For abnormal FIT, accountable follow-up and confirmed diagnostic resolution are feasible process targets. **Trial evidence:** Washington FQHC navigation 55.1% vs42.1% at12months (+13.0pp,95%CI6.5–19.4) in its specific population. Mixed-cancer Atlas trial full outreach/navigation package vs usual care +8.5pp (4.8–12.0) at120days, but adding navigation to outreach +0.4pp (-3.6–4.2). Do not add these estimates, extrapolate to a symptomatic30-year-old, or infer a stage/mortality effect.

**Endometriosis:** persistent symptoms and function must not be erased by normal examination/ultrasound; test criteria-based follow-through, pain/function, time to appropriate evaluation, patient experience and unnecessary intervention. UK NICE guidance is a contextual source, not a US legal standard or proof of a local failure.

**Rheumatoid arthritis:** where qualifying persistent synovitis is suspected, normal inflammatory markers/negative antibodies must not automatically override the clinical referral criteria; preserve alternate diagnoses and false referrals. Evaluate time to appropriate specialist review, function, harm and capacity. UK NICE guidance is not an automatic US referral rule.

## Systemwide recommendations, separated by proof strength

**Implementable research-data standard now:** preserve exact constructs, source and policy version, historical availability, observation time zero, patient/clinician visibility, uncertainty, actual comparator, and an explicit action-closure state. This is a data/decision integrity recommendation, not a clinical efficacy claim.

**Testable process changes:** cross-system longitudinal evidence view, symptom-aware standards crosswalk, outreach/closed-loop referral, network alternatives, staffing/capacity coordination and feedback. Compare P0 competent existing practice to P1 information integration, P2 reviewed recognition, P3 verified follow-through, P4 feedback; do not presume P4 is superior or that component effects add.

**Requires partner and governance:** local workflow audits, individual longitudinal records, within-county cross-organization comparisons, clinician/patient adjudication, prospective action, and outcome evaluation. Compare designed policy, operational delivery and experienced care; adjust for risk, selection, insurance, severity, access and shared specialty resources.

## Claim assurance and independent review

- Claim A: two-county aggregate burden contrast — **reproduced from retained source snapshot; original archive acquisition/provenance exception remains**. Reviewer must check exact numerator/denominator, date, geography, stage and suppression.
- Claim B: published screening guidance differs — **source-level fact; local adoption/impact unknown**. Reviewer must inspect exact versions and exceptions.
- Claim C: selected navigation interventions improve follow-up — **population-specific randomized evidence**. Reviewer must verify effect, CI, population, comparator and follow-up; clinical mortality/stage not measured.
- Claim D: fragmentation or restricted networks caused local late-stage excess — **unproven hypothesis**. Needs linked care trajectories, actual policy/network exposure, comparison and confounding analysis.
- Claim E: DIS–RPLICE changes local outcomes — **not demonstrated**. Requires independent decision experiment, then separately authorized field evaluation.

**Disconfirmation:** if equal-evidence, equal-time decisions are not better, if local follow-through is already reliable, if alerts add burden without completed care, if stage trends are driven by incidence/ascertainment, or if outside-network routes worsen timeliness, revise the intervention rather than preserve the favored explanation.

## Communication-ready statement

> Our first comparison found that two counties can have almost the same proportion of late-stage colorectal cancer cases while experiencing substantially different population rates of late-stage disease. That tells us why looking only at a headline percentage can hide unequal burden. Other studies show that some outreach and navigation programs improve completion of recommended diagnostic follow-up, while simply adding alerts or another service component does not always help. We are now testing which standards, information gaps, care handoffs and resource constraints explain variation in actual settings, and which changes improve appropriate completed care without creating new harm. We have not yet established that any particular organization caused the county difference or that our platform has reduced late diagnoses.
