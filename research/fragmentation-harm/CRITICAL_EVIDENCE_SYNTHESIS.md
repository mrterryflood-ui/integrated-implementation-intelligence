# Critical evidence synthesis: fragmentation versus completed intervention
Date: 2026-11-08. Search: Undermind /Fragmented clinical evidence and preventable harm causal mechanisms, completed 2026-11-08. 276 ranked papers identified. **Search summary is abstract/metadata based; this is not a completed systematic review or risk-of-bias assessment.**

## What evidence permits us to say
1. A multilevel primary-care intervention improved timely follow-up of abnormal cancer screening in a cluster randomized trial [Atl23]. Search synthesis reports ~8.5 percentage-point increase in 120-day follow-up. Verify original outcome definition, confidence interval, cluster analysis, and subgroup effects in full text before citing a definitive effect.
2. Patient navigation after abnormal stool screening improved 1-year colonoscopy completion in an RCT [Cor25]. Search synthesis reports ~13 percentage-point increase; verify the exact intention-to-treat estimand and CI from full text.
3. Longer time to colonoscopy after positive fecal testing is associated with adverse cancer outcomes in a systematic review [For20]; confounding, indication and selection into colonoscopy prevent interpreting the association as a precise causal effect. A nationwide French cohort reports a materially different pattern among those eventually undergoing colonoscopy [Gra25], underscoring selection and context.
4. Registry linkage can improve validity of EHR-based cancer research [Tho20]; EHR-derived public-health surveillance indicators have data completeness and semantic limitations [Con22b].
5. Human and organizational handoffs matter: root cause analysis of safety-net colonoscopy barriers [Sha20b]. Reminder/decision-support approaches have mixed results [Atl25c, Zub24].

## Evidence grading for the DIS–RPLICE program
| Claim | Current strength | Boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Multilevel navigation and follow-up systems can improve completion | Moderate–high for specific studied settings | Not necessarily a mortality effect |

## Research model and competing pathways
**Exposure:** access to timely, semantically harmonized, provenance-traceable evidence *plus* an accountable follow-up workflow, versus current workflow.
**Intermediate mechanisms:** abnormal-result identification, information availability, correct interpretation, outreach, scheduling, referral completion, colonoscopy, diagnosis.
**Patient outcomes:** stage at diagnosis, avoidable emergency presentation, treatment timeliness, patient-reported burden, and eventual morbidity/mortality, with adequate follow-up.
**Potential harms:** alert fatigue, false-positive escalation, increased workload, inequitable targeting, privacy/linkage error, opportunity costs.
**Confounding:** baseline disease incidence, risk, access, capacity, screening eligibility, social determinants, registry coding, migration and selection into diagnostic testing.

## Candidate evaluation
Pragmatic cluster-randomized or stepped-wedge rollout with no withdrawal of usual care. Primary endpoint: documented timely completion of follow-up after abnormal screening, using a prespecified window. Secondary endpoints: diagnostic resolution time, equity-stratified completion, false alerts, staff workload, patient experience. Longer-term stage and cancer outcomes exploratory unless sufficiently powered and linked. Require privacy, ethics and clinical governance.

## Key distinction
The Hinds/Harris difference in late-stage incidence with similar late-stage share **proves only that those two summary measures tell different descriptive stories**. It does not prove that any specific person had a missed intervention, that integration would have changed a decision, or that intervention would have prevented harm. To test that bridge, acquire pathway-level process data with explicit permissions and a prespecified counterfactual.
