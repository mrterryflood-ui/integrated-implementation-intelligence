# ChainWeb all-source prevention evidence: availability and decision-time audit
**As-of:** 2026-11-08 | **Pilot units:** Hinds County MS (FIPS 28049), Harris County TX (FIPS 48201) | **Scope:** source provenance and feasibility, **not** downloaded county-level exposure or patient observations.

## Why this matters
Issue #81 specifies an **integrated prevention** hypothesis, not just late-stage cancer detection. The test is whether independent epidemiologic, biological, environmental, social, clinical-capacity and experienced data can change a specific prevention decision *when they are actually available*. Evidence from a later release cannot retrospectively become an early warning in a 2019–2023 decision. Similarly, joining datasets by county FIPS does not create a patient-level cohort.

## Two legitimate, different target studies
**Retrospective descriptive reconstruction (now feasible, with caution):** for 2019–2023, study whether counties differ in outcome metrics and potentially relevant domain-level risks using consistent versions and a source-by-source comparability matrix. Later released data may be used for *retrospective description*, but not to assert they could have triggered a historical intervention. Some later revised sources may introduce post-period information; label version/revision.

**Prospective/decision-time study (not yet feasible):** obtain a real, documented decision date, stakeholder objective and evidence actually available before that date. Compare competent current practice with the integrated evidence account, blind and independently adjudicate warranted action vs justified nonchange. For clinical effect, separately evaluate delivered intervention and outcomes; never impute benefit from missingness or a risk score.

## Dependency and time guards
1. Define observation_start/end, event_timestamp, recording_timestamp, first_public_release_timestamp, retrieved_timestamp and decision_timestamp separately. Unknown release date => unavailable for a historical as-of reconstruction until verified.
2. Record FIPS, source population, numerator, denominator, instrument, stage coding, subgroup definitions, revision cycle, suppression, uncertainty and cross-county comparability status.
3. Do not pool age-adjusted rates with count-based case shares, modelled prevalence or program completion; contextual measures may remain side-by-side.
4. Track data lineage: NCI and CDC visualizations may represent the same registry submission. Corroboration requires independent primary data generation, not multiple dashboards.
5. Treat environmental exposures, health workforce, demographics, behavioral risks and patient barriers as alternative explanations requiring study, not presumed mechanisms.
6. Avoid claiming a 2026 report establishes what health systems or public officials **should have known in 2023**.

## Decision and completion
**VERIFIED:** authoritative cross-domain data sources exist and have fundamentally different units and availability times. **VERIFIED:** the retained cancer rates and case shares are different estimands (quantitative audit linked in PR #85). **UNKNOWN:** the Hinds/Harris risk-pattern contrasts in those additional domains, their decision relevance, and the true clinical pathway counterfactual. **NOT ESTABLISHED:** multisource integration would have prevented any cancer case or corrected any documented Hinds/Harris 2019–2023 recommendation.

**Next scientific acquisition unit:** one specified, dated prevention decision with an identifiable actual owner and target population. Then enumerate only data actually available at that time, determine whether later aggregate context would change current prioritization, and collect site-authorized process and patient-experience data for clinical effectiveness. Primary blockers are evidence access and prospective design, not another demo or code interface.
