The review reports evidence of medication safety, patient safety and cost benefits in some settings, while emphasizing the need for standardized research outcomes. https://scispace.com/papers/the-impact-of-electronic-health-record-interoperability-on-363q1exc

Powell et al., *Assessment of Health Information Technology-Related Outpatient Diagnostic Delays in the US Veterans Affairs Health Care System: A Qualitative Study of Aggregated Root Cause Analysis Data* (2020). Root-cause reports identify HIT-related diagnostic delay mechanisms. This is selected-event qualitative evidence, not a population causal effect. https://scispace.com/papers/assessment-of-health-information-technology-related-13l4a1zqyl
3. Dixit et al., *Electronic Health Record Use Issues and Diagnostic Error: A Scoping Review and Framework*, Journal of Patient Safety (2023). Human-systems integration lens links EHR usability/workflow issues with diagnostic processes; cannot alone quantify prevented harms. https://scispace.com/papers/electronic-health-record-use-issues-and-diagnostic-error-a-nvxmycgc
4. Huang et al., *Target trial emulation in oncology: current use and future directions*, Military Medical Research (2025). Reviews use of observational target-trial emulation and limitations of registry data and agreement with trials. https://scispace.com/papers/target-trial-emulation-in-oncology-current-use-and-future-rp4pcxvoowai
5. Ung, Hernán, Dahabreh, *Using the target trial framework for combining information: external comparator analyses and other applications* (2026). A framework for specifying target population and data-element alignment when combining heterogeneous sources; verify final publication and full text before methodological adoption. https://scispace.com/papers/using-the-target-trial-framework-for-combining-information-11ws15fcg40b

## Synthesis: evidence tiers
- **Supported general concern:** health information technology design, workflow and interoperability problems can contribute to safety and diagnostic process failures.
- **Partially supported mechanism:** fragmented documentation and weak handoffs can plausibly delay recognition or follow-up; magnitude varies by context and data source.
- **Not established in our pilots:** fragmentation caused Hinds/Harris cancer incidence or late-stage differences, that any particular referral was missed, or that integrating the current public datasets would prevent patient harm.
- **Evaluation requirement:** collect patient/pathway or auditable organizational process data, define feasible actions and counterfactuals, test for unintended consequences.

## Red-team questions
