# ChainWeb: upstream signs and prediagnostic missed opportunities
Date: 2026-11-08. Research correction to issue #81. Not clinical guidance or a validated patient-level model.

## Scope correction
Earlier work overemphasized post-positive-FIT referral closure. The primary investigation must also start **before screening**, including pre-eligibility family history, inherited risk, anemia, rectal bleeding, bowel changes, unexplained symptoms, contextual risk, previous encounters, and opportunities for clinically appropriate diagnostic workup. Screening asymptomatic populations and diagnostic evaluation of symptoms are different clinical decisions with different denominators and risk-benefit thresholds.

## Initial primary-study evidence (abstract-level extraction; full-text verification pending)
- [Fri23] Fritz et al. J Natl Cancer Inst (2023). doi:10.1093/jnci/djad068. Matched case-control: 5,075 early-onset CRC cases; abdominal pain, rectal bleeding, diarrhea and iron deficiency anemia associated with early-onset CRC 3–24 months before diagnosis. Odds ratios for individual signs 1.34–5.13; one, two, >=3 signs OR 1.94, 3.59, 6.52. These are *relative odds*, not predictive values or proven missed opportunities. About 19.3% of cases had first signs 3–24 months before diagnosis; this case fraction cannot be interpreted as a proportion of all symptomatic patients who have CRC.
- [Dem20] Demb et al. Gut (2020). doi:10.1136/gutjnl-2020-321849. VA matched cohorts age 18–49: IDA group 5-year CRC incidence 0.45% versus 0.05%, absolute difference 0.39 percentage points; hematochezia 0.33% versus 0.03%, difference 0.30 points. Association and absolute risk both matter. Cohort predominantly VA patients, transportability must be tested.
- [Chu26] Chubak et al. Cancer Epidemiol Biomarkers Prev (2026). doi:10.1158/1055-9965.EPI-25-0697. 28,198 adults 42–49 with diagnostic colonoscopy indication in three systems. PPV for blood loss 2.4% (95% CI 2.2–2.7); positive fecal testing 4.5% (3.3–5.9). Selected colonoscopy population, **not** population-wide PPV or screening threshold.
- [Dig25] Digby et al. Gut (2025). doi:10.1136/gutjnl-2024-334248. Symptomatic primary care cohort: 34,647 fecal hemoglobin results, 571 incident CRC; combining fecal hemoglobin, age and iron-deficiency anemia yielded substantial risk variation. Example: 30% (19.4–41.0) with fecal hemoglobin >99 µg/g, age >55 and IDA. Retrospective single-center risk stratification; calibration and external validation needed.

## Early-signal pathway and exact decision questions
1. **Prediagnostic signal availability:** Was a family-history or symptom signal recorded? If not, was it never asked, unavailable, documented elsewhere, or suppressed in extract?
2. **Construct validity:** Was anemia correctly classified using age/sex and laboratory thresholds? Was bleeding attributed to a plausible alternative cause, and was that explanation revisited?
3. **Temporal admissibility:** What information and clinical guidance were available *before* the decision? Do not backfill future diagnosis or future registry records.
4. **Decision opportunity:** Would an earlier evaluation have been indicated under contemporary standards, taking age, symptoms, prior history and patient preferences into account?
5. **Clinical action and capacity:** Was referral ordered, accepted, scheduled, completed, or declined? Were out-of-system results captured?
6. **Counterfactual:** Compare plausible earlier diagnostic action versus usual care among comparable eligible symptomatic people, with symptom severity, access, healthcare utilization and selection addressed.
7. **Outcome and harms:** Measure diagnostic interval, stage, patient-relevant outcomes, unnecessary testing, false positives, procedure complications, cost and inequity. Do not assume an alert prevented cancer.

## Distinguish three populations
- Asymptomatic people eligible for routine screening;
- Symptomatic people requiring diagnostic evaluation regardless of routine screening age;
- People with hereditary/familial or other high-risk indications needing individualized surveillance.

Do not pool these populations or apply a single test threshold to all.

## Falsification and cross-disease transfer
Negative control: signals documented only after cancer diagnosis cannot be treated as prospectively available. Sensitivity analyses must address ascertainment/encounter frequency, differential lab testing, symptoms recorded during diagnostic workup, selection into colonoscopy, and lead-time bias. If earlier signals have poor positive predictive value, no actionable intervention window, or cause unacceptable false positives, the broad early-detection claim is weakened.

Transferable *method*: reconstruct longitudinal signal -> interpretation -> owner -> action -> outcome with valid source timing and patient experience. Transferable *clinical threshold*: NOT established; investigate lung cancer and women's health separately.

## Scientific decision
Strong evidence that certain early symptoms and laboratory signals are associated with CRC and could motivate earlier evaluation. **No evidence yet** that the DIS–RPLICE system identified those signals prospectively, prevented a delayed diagnosis, or would improve population outcomes. Prioritize this upstream investigation over more post-FIT demonstrations. Deep Undermind search launched for full synthesis.
