## 4. Distinct clocks and whole cohorts

Separate patient-experienced onset, first recorded signal, first clinician-available signal, decision, action and outcome. Separately retain observation period, publication/revision/retrieval dates and historical availability. Use only information knowable at the decision time. Include never-diagnosed and unresolved episodes rather than selecting only people who eventually received a diagnosis.

Symptomatic diagnosis, hereditary surveillance, asymptomatic risk-adapted screening and primary prevention require different eligibility and time-zero definitions. Age is not a software veto on investigating a warning sign; this does not mean screening every age or applying an unvalidated test threshold.

## 6. Three linked evaluations

**A. Decision-quality experiment:** freeze a source-grounded casebook and rubric before evaluating. Compare DIS–RPLICE with competent current practice at the same decision question, evidence cutoff, review time and resource allowance. Hold out evaluation cases from prompt/model development; blind adjudicators and address carryover where feasible. Measure appropriate recognition/action, unsupported escalation, honest uncertainty, feasibility, patient/context inclusion, workload and cost. Include justified nonchanges and null cases. A scenario is not a real historical missed opportunity; actual historic claims require dated recommendations and their input record. Set sample size using the meaningful difference, variability and clustering rather than calling a small feasibility set an efficacy study.

**B. First-signal pathway evaluation:** specify eligibility, time zero, competing strategies, follow-up, confounders, competing events and ascertainment before outcome review. Retain the full outcome vector. Obtain authorized longitudinal records and an appropriate privacy/ethics determination for local patient-level inference; public aggregates do not identify those effects.

**C. ChainWeb evaluation:** define each proposed direct, mediated, between-service spillover or sustained system effect, with population, comparator, observation window, harms and disconfirmation. Do not double-count benefits. Connected benefits are not prerequisites for reporting a bounded software test; they are prerequisites for claiming connected prevention effectiveness.

Apply measurement, temporal, causal, operational and transfer pressure tests to the platform's own scoring as well as its scientific claims. A failed gate constrains the affected claim, not unrelated lawful research.
