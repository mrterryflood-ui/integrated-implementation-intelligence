# ChainWeb cross-system standardization and actionable counterfactuals — primary-evidence synthesis 2026-11-08

**Authority:** Owner's systems-of-systems thesis. **Status:** Primary-source-backed research synthesis and test specification; not a local patient-level causal result or a live intervention. **Mission:** DIS/RPLICE are the drivers; CRC calibrates the method; endometriosis and autoimmune pathways are parallel scale-out; HerHealth and mental-health catalogs provide tertiary context, not independent confirmation. Dartmouth's Monday partnership accelerator is separate.

## Thesis partition: known, measured, and not yet proven
**Known/observable:** different organizations and published instruments use different measures, referral designs, capacities and rules. Differences can be measured, and measurement equivalence cannot be presumed. **Testable mechanism:** restrictive in-network referral rules, handoff fragmentation, guideline interpretation, missed symptom recognition and capacity may interact to delay appropriate diagnostic resolution. **Causal benefit still to establish:** a shared operating picture + construct crosswalk + clinically appropriate escalation + accountable cross-boundary closure improves decisions, time to resolution, stage-specific burden, patient experience, equity and harms versus competent local practice. Closed networks can also aid coordination; test their net effect, not an assumed direction.

## Systems-of-systems counterfactual model
Index a patient/episode at the earliest **historically knowable** risk or warning signal, with distinct patient-experienced onset and clinician-visible timestamps. Include people without eventual disease and those whose outcome is unresolved. Set exposure to the *operational policy package*, not merely an EHR flag:
- P0: competent observed current practice, including local rules, referral boundaries, available services and follow-through.
- P1: standards/construct crosswalk and longitudinal shared operating picture; no new clinical intervention.
- P2: P1 plus risk- and symptom-appropriate evaluation prompts, transparent uncertainty and explicit clinician review.
- P3: P2 plus accountable cross-network referral/diagnostic resolution, capacity/insurance navigation, patient preference and confirmation of completed action.
- P4: P3 plus continuous feedback, drift monitoring, safety-netting, subgroup review and resource adaptation.
P1–P4 are **separable counterfactual intervention packages**, not observed clinical treatments or guaranteed monotonic improvements. Each may introduce alert fatigue, overdiagnosis, extra burden, data leakage or specialist bottlenecks. Interactions can make combined effects smaller or larger than sums of isolated components.

## What must be measured at every transition
1. **Recognize:** proportion of eligible episodes with contemporaneously observable signals recognized, time-to-recognition, and false alarms; stratify by age, sex, race/ethnicity where lawful, insurance, language, deprivation, clinic and source availability.
2. **Decide:** guideline-appropriate evaluation versus no action; compare multiple published standards using an explicit versioned crosswalk, not forced uniformity; document clinician rationale and patient preference.
3. **Act:** orders, completed procedures, diagnostic resolution, referrals outside the home network, denials, wait times, capacity and costs; record lost-to-follow-up and external care as separate states.
4. **Outcomes:** incident disease, stage-specific counts/rates and early:late ratios, emergency diagnosis, mortality where feasible, function, symptoms, quality of life, adverse events, inequity, cost and sustainability; avoid interpreting lead-time, length bias, stage migration or precursor removal as causal improvement.
5. **ChainWeb:** specify mediator and spillover edges (e.g., symptom recognition -> referral -> diagnostic resolution -> timely therapy), distinguish causal claims from observed dependencies, test for negative effects in other pathways, avoid double-counting.

## Site and standards comparison design
A same-county comparison across FQHC, hospital-affiliated, independent and government-operated care settings is an important proposed study. **None is enrolled.** Match/reweight for case mix, symptom severity, age, insurance, prior encounters, referral eligibility, baseline risk, capacity and neighborhood context; explicitly measure selection into health systems and shared specialists. Include patients who never completed referral. Measure network restrictions and outside-network availability rather than assuming all referrals are in-system. Separate Designed policy, Operational workflows and Experienced patient accounts. Consider stepped implementation or controlled interrupted time series only if ethical and feasible; otherwise target-trial emulation with defensible confounding/sensitivity analysis. Two clinics alone cannot identify a system-level causal effect.

## Decision rule
Do not standardize away appropriate condition-specific clinical discretion. Standardize **meaning, temporal provenance, decision transparency, closed-loop status, and outcome definitions**, then test whether local processes need modification. For every identified fault, record owner, evidence, root-cause rival, feasible correction, comparison, adverse effects, closure test and follow-up. No single 'standardization' score can establish benefit.
